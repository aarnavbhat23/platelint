## 2. Materials and Methods
2. Materials and Methods 2.1. Cell Culture Murine 4T1 (ATCC ® CRL-2539™) cells were maintained in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM; Corning Incorporated, Corning, NY, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; Gibco, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA), 100 U/mL penicillin and 100 µg/mL streptomycin (Corning Incorporated, Corning, NY, USA). Cell cultures were incubated (Sheldon Manufacturing, Inc., Cornelius, OR, USA) at 37 °C in a humidified atmosphere with 85% relative humidity and 5% CO 2 until reaching 80–90% confluence. 2.2. Preparation of TYE563 A working solution of the TYE563 ® -labeled oligonucleotide (Integrated DNA Technologies, Inc., Coralville, IA, USA) was prepared at a concentration of 1000 nM in nuclease-free water (Integrated DNA Technologies, Inc., Coralville, IA, USA) to facilitate subsequent dilutions for the 5 nM and 10 nM working concentrations used in the assays. This fluorophore exhibits an excitation wavelength of 550 nm and an emission wavelength of 563 nm. 2.3. Preparation of Xfect For all experiments, Xfect™ RNA Transfection Polymer (Takara Bio USA, Inc., San Jose, CA, USA) was used at a stock concentration of 100 µg/µL. The reagent was employed directly at a 1:1 volumetric ratio ( v / v ) relative to the volume of TYE563 working solution used in each assay. 2.4. Preparation of PEI Polyethylenimine (PEI, branched/linear, 40 kDa; Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) solutions were prepared based on the molar concentration of its repeating monomeric units (MW = 43.07 g/mol), which provides a standardized measure of cationic amine density for nucleic acid complexation. Briefly, 0.323 g of PEI was dissolved in double-distilled water, and the final volume was adjusted to 50 mL to yield a 150 mM stock solution (in terms of monomeric units). A 10 mM working solution was subsequently prepared by diluting 66 µL of the stock solution in 934 µL of double-distilled water. This 10 mM working solution was used at a 1:1 volumetric ratio ( v / v ) relative to the volume of the TYE563 working solution applied in all cellular uptake assays ( Figure 1 ). 2.5. Preparation of Low-Molecular-Weight Chitosan Solution A 0.3% ( w / v ) working solution of low-molecular-weight chitosan (50–190 kDa; Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA, according to the manufacturer’s specifications) was prepared in 0.1 M acetic acid. Briefly, the required volume of acetic acid was placed in a beaker, after which chitosan powder was added under continuous stirring for 24 h to ensure complete dissolution. The resulting solution was stored at 4 °C until further use ( Figure 1 ). 2.6. Synthesis of Polyethylenimine-Reduced Gold Nanoparticles Gold nanoparticles were synthesized using HAuCl 4 as the metal precursor and polyethylenimine (PEI) as the reducing agent. A 2 mM HAuCl 4 solution was prepared in double-distilled water and used in a 1:0.1 synthesis ratio with PEI. Briefly, 5 mL of 2 mM HAuCl 4 solution was placed in a beaker and heated in a water bath at 100 °C for 5 min (VWR). Subsequently, 500 µL of PEI at a concentration of 100 mM was added. The beaker was covered with aluminum foil and maintained under constant stirring and temperature for approximately 10 min. Following this incubation period, the formation of gold nanoparticles was indicated by a visible color change to dark purple. After the color change was observed, the beaker was removed from the water bath and allowed to cool to room temperature. The resulting nanoparticle suspension was filtered through a 0.2 µm membrane filter and stored under refrigeration until further use ( Figure 1 ). 2.7. Synthesis of Chitosan-Reduced Gold Nanoparticles Gold nanoparticles were synthesized using HAuCl 4 as the metal precursor and low-molecular-weight chitosan as the reducing agent. A 2 mM solution of HAuCl 4 was prepared in double-distilled water, while chitosan was prepared at 1% ( w / v ) in 0.1 M acetic acid. The synthesis was performed using a 3:1 ratio of HAuCl 4 to chitosan. Briefly, 6 mL of the 2 mM HAuCl 4 solution was placed in a beaker and heated in a water bath at 100 °C (VWR). Immediately afterward, 2 mL of 1% low-molecular-weight chitosan solution was added. The reaction mixture was maintained under these conditions for approximately 15 min. Successful nanoparticle formation was indicated by a visible color change from yellow to a wine-red solution within the reaction time ( Figure 1 ). 2.8. Characterization of Gold Nanoparticles The synthesized gold nanoparticles functionalized with polyethyleneimine (AuPEI) and chitosan (AuCHI) were characterized in terms of hydrodynamic diameter and surface charge using dynamic light scattering (DLS) and zeta potential measurements, respectively. These parameters were selected because they are directly relevant to the colloidal behavior of the nanocarriers and their potential interaction with negatively charged oligonucleotides ( Supplementary Materials ). 2.9. Preparation of Delivery Vehicle Complexes A key step in the complexation process between the delivery agents and the TYE563 ® oligonucleotide was the initial dilution with 150 mM NaCl, which facilitated electrostatic self-assembly. In all cases, complexes were prepared using a 1:1 volumetric ratio between the delivery agent and TYE563 ® , regardless of the final TYE563 ® concentration (5 nM or 10 nM). Five delivery carriers were evaluated: polyethylenimine (PEI; Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA), Xfect (Takara Bio USA, Inc., San Jose, CA, USA), chitosan (CHI), PEI–gold nanoparticle conjugates (AuPEI), and chitosan–gold nanoparticle conjugates (AuCHI). A fluorescently labeled oligonucleotide (5′-TYE563 ® ; Integrated DNA Technologies, Inc., Coralville, IA, USA) was used as a reporter cargo to evaluate cell-associated uptake. Complexes were formed by mixing each delivery agent with 5′-TYE563 ® at a 1:1 mass ratio, followed by volume adjustment with 150 mM NaCl. To evaluate the effect of complex assembly time, mixtures were incubated under medium-intensity vortex agitation for either 15 s or 15 min. The detailed volumes, stock concentrations, and final working concentrations for the preparation of the delivery vehicle complexes are presented in Table 1 . 2.10. 4T1 Delivery Protocols Cells were subjected to two distinct delivery approaches, conventionally referred to in the literature as standard (adherent) and reverse (suspension) delivery approaches. In this study, these protocols were employed specifically to compare physical cellular uptake dynamics rather than functional gene expression. 2.11. Standard (Adherent) Delivery Approach Cells were seeded in 24-well plates at a density of approximately 10,000 cells per well in 500 µL of complete DMEM supplemented with fetal bovine serum (FBS). After reaching 80–90% confluence, cell monolayers were rinsed with PBS. Subsequently, 300 µL of the preformed delivery vehicle complex was added, and the final volume was adjusted to 500 µL using fresh culture medium. Plates were incubated for 24 h at 37 °C. Complexes were assembled using two vortex-homogenization times (15 s or 15 min) prior to addition to the adherent monolayer. 2.12. Reverse (Suspension) Delivery Approach When cultures reached 80–90% confluence in flasks, cells were washed with PBS and dissociated using 0.25% trypsin–EDTA (Gibco, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) for 5–10 min at 37 °C. The enzymatic reaction was neutralized with serum-supplemented medium. Suspended cells were centrifuged at 343× g for 5 min, and the resulting pellet was resuspended in fresh culture medium. Cell density and viability were determined using the Trypan Blue exclusion assay. An aliquot containing approximately 10,000 cells was transferred to each well of a 24-well plate already containing the pre-deposited delivery vehicle complexes (5 nM or 10 nM TYE563 ® ). The mixture was gently homogenized, and 200 µL of culture medium was added to reach a final volume of 500 µL. As with the adherent approach, two complexation times were evaluated (15 s and 15 min), followed by a 24 h incubation period. 2.13. Cell Viability Assay Cell viability was assessed using Calcein AM as a fluorescent viability marker. A 1 mM stock solution was prepared by resuspending 50 µg of Calcein AM powder in 50 µL of DMSO. A working solution was subsequently prepared by diluting the stock in PBS to a final concentration of 10 µM. Following 24 h treatment with the delivery vehicle complexes in 24-well plates, the culture medium was removed, and cells were incubated with 10 µM Calcein AM in PBS for 30 min at 37 °C. The staining solution was then removed, and wells were washed twice with PBS to eliminate residual dye. Negative controls consisted of untreated cells without Calcein AM staining, whereas viability controls consisted of untreated cells stained with Calcein AM. Mean fluorescence intensity (MFI) of control wells, measured ImageJ version 1.54t (National Institutes of Health, USA; Java 1.8.0, represented 100% cell viability. Viability percentages for treated groups were calculated relative to the control group. Calcein AM exhibits excitation and emission wavelengths at 490 nm and 525 nm, respectively. 2.14. Cell-Associated Uptake Evaluation To determine cell-associated uptake efficiency, each delivery carrier was complexed with 5′-TYE563 ® at a 1:1 ratio. The objective was to trace the spatial localization of the fluorescent cargo and identify the system exhibiting the highest cell interaction. Dual-channel fluorescence microscopy was performed using an Olympus IX70 inverted microscope. Detection channels included green fluorescence for Calcein AM (viability, emission at 525 nm) and red fluorescence for TYE563 ® (cargo uptake, excitation/emission at 550/563 nm). Spectral separation ensured simultaneous visualization without channel crosstalk. 2.15. Fluorescence Image Analysis and Quantification Fluorescence images were processed using ImageJ version 1.54t (National Institutes of Health, USA; Java 1.8.0) under a standardized workflow. All images were acquired under identical exposure and gain settings. A uniform background subtraction was applied across all raw images. Linear adjustments were applied consistently to avoid green signal overflow into the red channel. Quantification of Mean Fluorescence Intensity (MFI): Each image was divided into four equal non-overlapping quadrants analyzed independently. A minimum of three biological replicates per condition were evaluated ( n = 12 measured fields per group). Quadrants devoid of cells were excluded. Cell-Associated Uptake Analysis (Colocalization Approach): Uptake efficiency was determined by analyzing the colocalization of Calcein AM (green) and TYE563 ® (red) signals. Single-channel images were merged. Positive cell-associated uptake events were identified strictly when both signals colocalized within the same cytosolic space, generating a merged yellow/orange signal. Cell Counting and Normalization: The number of double-positive (Calcein AM + /TYE563 + ) cells was quantified manually or using semi-automated counting tools in ImageJ version 1.54t (National Institutes of Health, USA; Java 1.8.0). Methodological Considerations This image-based quantification approach allows simultaneous evaluation of cell viability and nanoparticle-mediated delivery within the same sample, without disrupting the culture. Additionally, the use of quadrant-based sampling increases statistical robustness while maintaining spatial representativity. Importantly, the spectral separation between Calcein AM (emission: ~525 nm) and TYE563 (excitation: ~550 nm) minimizes channel overlap, enabling reliable co-localization analysis. 2.17. Statistical Analysis To determine the presence of statistically significant differences in cell viability, mean fluorescence intensity, and the percentage of interaction between delivery agents and 4T1 cells, data normality was first verified using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Subsequently, a one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was performed using the mean values of each experimental group, followed by Tukey’s post hoc test for multiple comparisons between treatments. All statistical analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism (11.0.0) and a p -value of p < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.
