FreeZone 4.5 Experimental model and study participant details Primary cell cultures and cell lines Rat bone marrow mesenchymal stem cells (rBMSCs, derived from male Sprague-Dawley rats) and the RAW 264.7 murine macrophage cell line (derived from a male BALB/c mouse) were purchased from Cyagen Biosciences (Guangzhou, China). rBMSCs were maintained in α-MEM supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin. Cells at passage 5 were harvested for bioink encapsulation and downstream osteogenic differentiation assays. RAW 264.7 macrophages were cultured in high-glucose DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS and 1% penicillin-streptomycin. All cultures were maintained at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO 2 and routinely tested to confirm the absence of mycoplasma contamination. Nude mouse subcutaneous model Eight-week-old female BALB/c nude mice (athymic) were purchased from Changzhou Cavens Laboratory Animal Co., Ltd. (Changzhou, China). Animals were housed in a specific-pathogen-free (SPF) facility under standardized environmental conditions (temperature: 22°C–24°C; relative humidity: 50%–60%; 12 h light/12 h dark cycle) with free access to a sterile standard rodent diet and water. Animals were randomly allocated to experimental groups for the ectopic subcutaneous implantation of bioprinted bone organoid precursors. All animal handling, surgical procedures, and post-operative care were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Shanghai University (Approval No. ECSHU 2024-127) and performed in strict accordance with institutional animal care guidelines and ARRIVE standards. Rat calvarial defect model Eight-week-old female Sprague-Dawley (SD) rats (body weight: 200–220 g; total n = 48) were purchased from Changzhou Cavens Laboratory Animal Co., Ltd. (Changzhou, China). The rats were housed in the SPF barrier facility under controlled ambient conditions (temperature: 22°C–24°C; relative humidity: 50%–60%; 12 h light/12 h dark cycle) with ad libitum access to standard chow and water. Female animals were utilized across all cohorts to maintain physiological and baseline consistency in bone regeneration. Rats were randomly assigned to treatment and control cohorts ( n = 6 per group at 4 and 8 weeks post-implantation). All surgical protocols, defect creation, and harvest procedures were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Shanghai University (Approval No. ECSHU 2024-127) and complied with institutional and national regulations for laboratory animal use. Method details Extraction of aloe vera gel Fresh, mature leaves of Aloe vera var. barbadensis Miller were thoroughly washed with sterile deionized water and surface-sterilized under ultraviolet light for 1 h in a laminar flow hood. Under aseptic conditions, the external green epidermis was peeled using a sterile scalpel, and the inner parenchymatous gel was collected by scraping the hydrogel core. The crude gel was filtered through sterile gauze to remove particulate debris, stirred continuously at room temperature for 2 h to achieve uniform homogenization, and centrifuged at 8000 rpm for 15 min at room temperature to eliminate residual impurities. The clear supernatant was harvested as purified Aloe vera (AV) gel. Gel permeation chromatography (GPC) and liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS) were conducted to verify the polysaccharide profile and identify small-molecule bioactive constituents. Synthesis and validation of GelMA Gelatin methacryloyl (GelMA) was synthesized by dissolving 10 g of gelatin in 100 mL of deionized water under continuous stirring (300 rpm) at 50°C to prepare a 10 wt % solution. While maintaining 50°C, 4 mL of methacrylic anhydride was added dropwise at a feed rate of 0.5 mL/min, and the mixture was allowed to react under continuous stirring for 3 h. The reaction was terminated by adding 300 mL of deionized water. The solution was dialyzed against a 13 kDa cutoff membrane at 37°C for 7 days, replacing the deionized water 2–3 times daily. The completeness of dialysis was monitored electrochemically until the conductivity of the dialysate matched that of pure deionized water and the pH stabilized within the neutral range. The dialyzed solution was frozen in liquid nitrogen for 24 h and lyophilized for 72 h to yield a white porous GelMA powder. Complete removal of unreacted methacrylic anhydride and methacrylic acid byproducts and an approximately 60% degree of substitution were confirmed by 1 H-NMR spectroscopy. Lyophilized GelMA was stored in a dry, light-shielded container at −20°C. Preparation of plant-based composite bioinks GelMA powder and Laponite nanoclay particles were sterilized under ultraviolet light for 2 h prior to bioink preparation. Three formulations were prepared under aseptic conditions. 1) GelMA bioink: 0.5 g of GelMA was dissolved in 5 mL of sterile deionized water containing 0.0125 g of lithium phenyl-2,4,6-trimethylbenzoylphosphinate (LAP) under continuous stirring at 37°C for 2 h to yield a 10 wt % GelMA bioink. 2) GelMA/Laponite bioink: 0.05 g of Laponite nanoclay was added to the 10 wt % GelMA solution and stirred at 37°C for 2 h until a homogeneous suspension was formed (10 wt % GelMA, 1 wt % Laponite). 3) GelMA/Laponite/AV bioink: 0.5 g of GelMA and 0.05 g of Laponite nanoclay were dispersed in 3 mL of sterile deionized water containing 0.0125 g of LAP. After 1 h of stirring at 37°C to ensure complete dispersion of Laponite without air bubbles, 2 mL of pre-prepared sterile Aloe vera gel was added and stirred for an additional 1 h at 37°C (final ratio: 10 wt % GelMA, 1 wt % Laponite, and 40 vol % AV). 3D bioprinting of bone organoids precursors Construct architecture was designed in Autodesk 3ds Max as an 8-layer rectangular porous grid (5 mm × 5 mm) with an infill spacing of 1.5 mm and an orthogonal 90° interlayer rotation applied twice. rBMSCs at passage 5 were detached with 0.25% trypsin-EDTA, centrifuged (1000 g, 5 min), and gently resuspended in pre-warmed (37°C) bioinks at a density of 1.0×10 6 cells/mL. The cell-laden bioink was loaded into a light-shielded cartridge and printed using a low-temperature extrusion bioprinter (Bio-Architect SR) at 8°C. Printing parameters were set to: pneumatic pressure of 0.2–0.3 MPa, printing speed of 4 mm/s, retraction height of 2 mm, and a 21G metal nozzle. Filaments were crosslinked layer-by-layer under 365 nm UV light at 5% intensity (20 mW/cm 2 ) for 10 s per layer, followed by 30 s post-curing to stabilize the construct. Printability (Pr) and diffusion rate (Dfr) were calculated from stereomicrographs: Pr = π 4 × 1 c = L 2 16 A D f r = A t f − A a f A a f × 100 % where L is the pore perimeter, A is the pore area, C is circularity, At f is the theoretical pore area, and Aa f is the actual pore area. In vitro culture of bone organoids Immediately following printing, construct precursors were transferred into multi-well plates and incubated at 37°C in a humidified 5% CO 2 atmosphere. Constructs were cultured in growth medium (α-MEM containing 10% FBS and 1% penicillin-streptomycin) for the first 24 h. The medium was subsequently replaced with osteogenic induction medium consisting of α-MEM supplemented with 10% FBS, 1% penicillin-streptomycin, 10 mM β-glycerophosphate, 50 μg/mL L-ascorbic acid, and 100 nM dexamethasone. Cultures were maintained under static conditions for up to 28 days, with medium refreshed every 2–3 days. Cell viability and proliferation assays Cell viability within bioprinted constructs was assessed at days 1, 4, 7, and 14 using a Calcein-AM/propidium iodide (PI) detection kit. Constructs were washed three times with PBS and incubated with staining solution (Calcein-AM: PI: PBS = 1 μL: 1 μL: 1000 μL) at 37°C for 30 min in the dark. After three PBS washes, fluorescence images were acquired using a confocal laser scanning microscope (Olympus FV3000), and live/dead ratios were quantified using ImageJ software. Proliferation of encapsulated rBMSCs was evaluated on days 1, 4, 7, and 14 using a CCK-8 kit. Constructs were rinsed with PBS and incubated in 10% CCK-8 working solution prepared in culture medium at 37°C for 1 h protected from light. A 100 μL aliquot of the incubated supernatant was transferred into a 96-well plate, and absorbance at 450 nm was measured on a microplate reader. Alkaline phosphatase and Alizarin Red S staining For 2D co-culture evaluation, rBMSCs were seeded in 12-well plates and co-cultured with acellular scaffolds placed in Transwell inserts, using growth medium on day 1 and osteogenic induction medium from day 2 onward. On day 10, cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA) for 15 min and washed with PBS. Alkaline phosphatase (ALP) staining was performed using a BCIP/NBT chromogenic kit for 30 min according to the manufacturer’s protocol. For Alizarin Red S (ARS) staining, fixed cells were stained with ARS solution for 30 min and rinsed with PBS. For 3D bone organoids, constructs cultured for 14 and 28 days were fixed in 4% PFA for 15 min, embedded in paraffin wax, and sliced into 5 μm thick sections. Deparaffinized sections were stained with ARS or BCIP/NBT ALP staining solutions for 30 min, washed three times with PBS, and imaged on a microplate reader and optical microscope. Gene expression analysis by RT-qPCR On day 10 of osteogenic co-culture, cells were lysed by adding 500 μL of Trizol reagent per well, rocked to ensure complete coverage, and incubated on ice for 5 min. Chloroform (100 μL) was added, vortexed vigorously until milky-white, held on ice for 5 min, and centrifuged at 12000 g for 15 min at 4°C. The clear upper aqueous layer was transferred into a fresh microcentrifuge tube, mixed with an equal volume of isopropanol, incubated on ice for 10 min, and centrifuged at 12000 g for 10 min at 4°C. The RNA pellet was washed with 1 mL of 75% ethanol, centrifuged at 7500 g for 5 min at 4°C, air-dried for 10 min, and dissolved in RNase-free DEPC water. Concentration and purity were measured using a microplate reader. Complementary DNA was synthesized from 1000 ng of total RNA in a 20 μL reaction containing PrimeScript RT master mix (37°C for 15 min, 85°C for 15 s, and held at 4°C). RT-qPCR was performed using TB Green Premix Ex Taq on a qTOWER 3 system in a 20 μL reaction containing diluted cDNA and specific primers. Relative target gene expression was determined using the 2 −ΔΔCt method normalized against GAPDH. Protein analysis by western blot Following 10 days of osteogenic co-culture, adherent cells were washed with PBS and lysed in 200 μL of RIPA buffer containing protease inhibitors, phosphatase inhibitors, and EDTA on ice for 30 min. Lysates were centrifuged at 12000 g for 5 min at 4°C, and total protein concentration was measured using a BCA kit. Protein samples were mixed with 5× protein loading buffer at a 4:1 volume ratio and denatured at 100°C for 10 min. Equal amounts of protein (25 μg/lane) were loaded onto 10% SDS-PAGE gels, resolved at 80 V for 30 min and 120 V for 60 min, and electrotransferred onto methanol-activated PVDF membranes at 300 mA for 100 min in an ice-cooled tank. Membranes were blocked with protein-free rapid blocking buffer for 15 min and incubated overnight at 4°C with primary antibodies against Collagen type I (1:1000), RUNX2 (1:1000), and GAPDH (1:2000). Membranes were washed three times with TBST (10 min each) and incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies (1:5000) for 1.5 h at room temperature. Bands were visualized using an Omni-ECL chemiluminescence kit on a Bio-Rad imaging system. Micro-CT scanning and analysis Mineralization of in vitro constructs and in vivo bone repair was characterized using a SkyScan micro-CT scanner (SkyScan 1172/1272). Scanning parameters for subcutaneously implanted and in vitro-cultured bone constructs were adjusted to 65 kV and 80 μA, with an isotropic voxel size of 9 μm, a 0.5 mm aluminum filter to minimize beam hardening, and a rotation step of 0.4° over a total rotation of 360°. Cross-sectional images were reconstructed using NRecon software and analyzed with CTAn software. A uniform circular region of interest (ROI) centered on the construct was defined to encompass the volume. Bone volume fraction (BV/TV %), trabecular thickness (Tb.Th, μm), and trabecular number (Tb.N, 1/mm) were determined using auxiliary system software. Three-dimensional images were reconstructed using CTVoX software. Macrophage recruitment and invasion A Transwell co-culture system was used to evaluate macrophage migration. RAW 264.7 cells (1 × 10 5 cells) were seeded into the upper insert, while bone organoid scaffolds were placed in the lower chamber. The co-culture was incubated at 37°C for 24 h. Non-migrated cells remaining on the upper surface were removed using a sterile cotton swab, and cells that traversed the membrane to the underside were fixed with 4% PFA and stained with 0.5% crystal violet solution. For invasion assays, the upper insert was pre-coated with 0.1 wt % gelatin and incubated overnight at 4°C prior to seeding RAW 264.7 cells. Migrated and invaded cells were observed and imaged under an optical microscope. Macrophage adhesion and polarization For cell adhesion, 1 × 10 4 RAW 264.7 cells were seeded onto the surface of printed acellular scaffolds in multi-well plates. On days 1, 4, and 7, scaffolds were retrieved and subjected to Calcein-AM/PI viability staining. For macrophage polarization, RAW 264.7 cells (1 × 10 4 cells) were seeded on coverslips in the lower Transwell chamber, while scaffolds were placed in the upper insert. After 24 h of co-culture, coverslips were fixed with 4% PFA for 15 min, permeabilized with immunofluorescence permeabilization reagent (0.1% Triton X-100) for 15 min, blocked with ready-to-use goat serum for 30 min, and incubated with primary antibodies against iNOS (1:500) and CD206 (1:500) overnight at 4°C. Coverslips were then incubated with Alexa Fluor 488- and Alexa Fluor 594-conjugated secondary antibodies for 30 min, counterstained with DAPI for 5–20 min in the dark, and imaged on a confocal laser scanning microscope (Olympus FV3000). The sustained regulatory effect on macrophages was similarly evaluated by co-culturing scaffolds retrieved after 14 and 28 days of culture. Flow cytometry analysis of macrophages Following 24 h of Transwell co-culture, RAW 264.7 cells were detached with trypsin-EDTA, washed with PBS, and collected by centrifugation (1000 g, 5 min). Cells were fixed in 4% PFA for 10 min, permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100 for 10 min, and incubated with Alexa Fluor 488-conjugated anti-iNOS (1:100) and PE-conjugated anti-CD206 (1:100) antibodies for 30 min. After repeated washing with PBS, cells were analyzed on a flow cytometer to determine the percentages of iNOS + and CD206 + populations. Subcutaneous implantation in nude mice Eight-week-old female BALB/c nude mice were anesthetized with isoflurane (induction: 3%–5%, maintenance: 1%–2%). The dorsal region was disinfected with povidone-iodine, a midline incision was made, and bone organoid precursors were implanted subcutaneously before closing the incision. At 2 and 4 weeks post-surgery, mice were euthanized, and implants were retrieved for Micro-CT analysis, H&E staining, and immunofluorescence. Constructs were fixed in 4% PFA and decalcified in 10 wt % EDTA solution (pH 7.2–7.4) for 4 weeks. Sections (5 μm) were prepared from paraffin-embedded tissues and processed for H&E staining or blocked with 3% BSA for immunofluorescence staining. Sections were incubated overnight at 4°C with primary antibodies (OCN, RUNX2, Periostin, CD31, Collagen I, Vinculin, iNOS, and CD206, each diluted 1:500 in 3% BSA), followed by secondary antibody incubation (1:500 in 3% BSA) and confocal imaging. Bulk RNA sequencing and analysis Dorsal subcutaneous bone organoids harvested at 4 weeks post-surgery were subjected to RNA sequencing. Total RNA was isolated using Trizol, and polyadenylated mRNA was enriched using magnetic oligo(dT) beads and thermally fragmented. Fragments were reverse-transcribed into first-strand cDNA, followed by second-strand cDNA synthesis, end repair, 3′ end adenylation, adapter ligation, and RT-qPCR amplification. Sequencing libraries were sequenced on an Illumina platform. Biological replicate reproducibility was assessed using Pearson correlation coefficients in R. Differential expression analysis was performed using edgeR. Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) analyses were performed using hypergeometric tests to evaluate pathway enrichment among differentially expressed genes. Rat calvarial defect repair surgery Eight-week-old female SD rats were anesthetized by intraperitoneal injection of 1% chloral hydrate. The cranium was shaved and disinfected with povidone-iodine. A midline scalp incision was made, and bilateral 5 mm critical-sized calvarial defects were created using a circular trephine drill under continuous saline irrigation, taking care to avoid injury to the dura mater. 21 Bone organoid precursors were implanted into the defect sites, and the incision was sutured. Rats were euthanized at 4 and 8 weeks post-implantation via an overdose of pentobarbital sodium. Cranial specimens were harvested for Micro-CT scanning, decalcified in 10 wt % EDTA solution, sectioned, and processed for H&E staining and immunofluorescence staining (OCN, Runx2, CD206, and iNOS). In vivo biosafety evaluation At 8 weeks post-implantation, major internal organs (heart, liver, spleen, lungs, and kidneys) were harvested from SD rats, fixed in 4% PFA for 24–48 h, dehydrated, paraffin-embedded, sectioned at 5 μm thickness without decalcification, and stained with H&E to evaluate histological biosafety. Blood samples were collected at 4 weeks post-operation for routine hematological counts (white blood cells, red blood cells, and monocytes) and serum biochemical analysis (ALT, AST, BUN, and CREA).
