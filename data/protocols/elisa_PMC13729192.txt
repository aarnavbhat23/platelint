## MATERIALS AND METHODS
MATERIALS AND METHODS Preparation of higher-throughput FAMA plates The VZV strain was preserved by Beijing Wantai Biological Pharmacy Enterprise Co., Ltd., and MRC-5 cells were purchased from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC). The V-Oka strain of VZV was inoculated into well-maintained MRC-5 cells at a multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 0.01. Following incubation until 50–100% cytopathic effect (CPE) was observed, the culture supernatant was aspirated and discarded. The cells were digested with trypsin and centrifuged at 300 × g for 5 min, and the supernatant was discarded. The cell density was adjusted to 10,000–30,000 cells per well using complete culture medium, and 100 μL of the cell suspension was dispensed into each well of a 96-well cell culture plate. Fixatives, including acetone, glutaraldehyde, or formaldehyde, were added individually, and the plates were fixed at room temperature for 30 min. After fixation, the plates were washed five times with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) supplemented with 0.5% Tween 80, vacuum-sealed, and stored at ≤ −60℃. Random sampling was performed in triplicate, and Hoechst nuclear staining reagent (Beyotime Biotechnology, Shanghai, China) was used to stain cells for the evaluation of inter-plate homogeneity. Optimization of clinical sample detection method Clinical sera were inactivated at 56℃ for 30 min, then serially diluted twofold with PBS supplemented with different additives (e.g., sodium chloride, sucrose, mannitol, bovine serum albumin, human serum albumin, urea, heparin, and ammonium sulfate). The diluted sera were dispensed into the higher-throughput FAMA plates at 100 μL per well and incubated at 37℃ for 30, 60, or 70 min. After incubation, the plates were washed five times with an automated plate washer, followed by the addition of a goat anti-human IgG-fluorescein isothiocyanate (FITC) antibody (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA) and re-incubation at 37℃ for 30 or 60 min. The liquid was aspirated and discarded, and the plates were patted dry. Hoechst nuclear staining reagent was added and incubated at room temperature for 5 min, after which the plates were washed again before glycerol was added. Image acquisition and automated analysis were performed using several instruments: a conventional fluorescence microscope (Ti2-D-PD, Nikon, Japan) for small-scale morphological observation, a high-content microscope (Molecular Devices, San Jose, CA, USA) for automated higher-throughput single-cell analysis, an enzyme-linked immunospot analyzer (sPot, AID, Germany), and a Celigo microscope (200-BFFL-5C, Revvity, MA, USA) for rapid cell counting. Unless otherwise specified, all results shown were obtained using the high-content microscope. The antibody titer was defined as the highest dilution showing a complete fluorescent ring on the cell membrane according to international FAMA guidelines. The international reference standard NIBSC W1044 (VZV FAMA standard) was included in each experimental run. The run was considered valid only if the standard’s measured titer fell within the supplier-defined acceptable range; this allowed batch-to-batch calibration and assessment of inter-assay reproducibility. FAMA slide assay Clinical sera were inactivated at 56℃ for 30 min and serially diluted twofold with PBS. The diluted sera were dispensed onto FAMA slides at 25 μL per well and incubated at 37℃ for 30 min. After incubation, the slides were washed on a shaker at 140 rpm for 6 min, then allowed to stand for 4 min, and this process was repeated three times. Finally, the slides were repeatedly immersed in purified water three times and air-dried at room temperature. Goat anti-human IgG-FITC antibody was added, and after re-incubation at 37℃ for 30 min, the slides were washed using the same method. After air-drying at room temperature, glycerol was added, and the slides were covered with coverslips for mounting. Serum antibody titers were observed and recorded using a fluorescence microscope. Cut-off value determination A total of 106 serum samples negative for VZV antibodies by FAMA slide assay were identified during baseline screening of participants enrolled in a phase III clinical trial (approval no. 2023-002-02) at Hubei Provincial Center for Disease Control and Prevention and used in this study under the same ethical approval. Three independent experimenters performed detections using three different batches of reagents according to the method established in the Optimization of clinical sample detection method section. The detection data were analyzed using Minitab Statistical Software 21 (Minitab, LLC, PA, USA). First, the Anderson-Darling test was used to assess the normality of the detection data. If the data were normally distributed, the upper limit of the 95% confidence interval (CI) was calculated as the cut-off value; if not normally distributed, the detection data were sorted in ascending order, and the one-sided 95th percentile was used as the cut-off value. Method validation To fully verify the performance of the method and meet clinical trial requirements, with reference to ICH M10: Bioanalytical Method Validation and Study Sample Analysis ( 24 ) and Technical Guidelines for the Research of Antibody Analysis Methods in Vaccine Clinical Trials ( 25 ), comprehensive validation of specificity, selectivity, relative accuracy and precision, linearity, stability, and robustness was performed on the method established in the Optimization of clinical sample detection method section based on the concepts of quality by design (QbD) and analytical method lifecycle management. Specificity evaluation was conducted using four distinct sample combinations consisting of negative reference samples, 1:1 mixtures of negative and positive references, 1:1 mixtures of VZV virus solution and negative references, and 1:1 mixtures of VZV virus solution and positive references to systematically assess matrix effects and pathogen-specific detection performance. Negative reference serum samples were obtained from baseline screening of a phase III varicella vaccine trial (approval no. 2023-002-02) at Hubei Provincial Center for Disease Control and Prevention. Positive reference serum samples were sourced from a separate varicella and herpes zoster vaccine trial (ChiCTR20252239), in which post-vaccination serum samples tested positive by the FAMA slide assay. The validation criteria required consistent negative results for negative reference samples and positive results for positive reference samples, with no detectable interference of exogenous VZV virus on the accuracy of detection outcomes. Method selectivity was assessed using one clinically derived hemolyzed serum sample and one hyperlipidemic serum sample. Experimental positive samples containing 5% hemolysis and 2 mM triglycerides were prepared by blending qualified positive references with the hemolytic and hyperlipidemic sera, respectively. Independent detection of normal, hemolyzed, and hyperlipidemic sera was also performed to verify the anti-interference capability of the assay. The acceptance criterion stipulated that hemolysis and hyperlipidemia at the tested concentrations should not induce observable deviations in detection results. A nested experimental design was adopted for the validation of relative accuracy and precision. Serum samples with high (titers 8,192 and 2,048), medium (512 and 256), and low (32 and 16) VZV antibody titers were selected as test objects. Two independent operators conducted duplicate detections at four different time points using three batches of reagents, generating 48 replicate data points for each titer level. The predefined acceptance thresholds were set as follows: the relative bias and geometric coefficient of variation (GCV) for samples near the limit of detection should not exceed 25 and 20%, respectively; samples at other titer levels should have a relative bias below 20% and a GCV below 15%. Linearity was evaluated via serial dilution of high-titer positive serum samples. The actual antibody titer of each diluted sample was measured and linearly regressed against the theoretical titer values. A coefficient of determination ( R ²) of no less than 0.980 was defined as the qualified standard for favorable linear correlation. Stability assessment included thermal accelerated stability and freeze-thaw stability verification using the working reference standard (calibrated with the international reference standard NIBSC W1044). Nine aliquots of this standard were distributed, three per condition (2–8℃ storage, room temperature incubation, and freeze-thaw cycles). For thermal stability testing, detection reagents and reference samples were stored at 2–8℃ for 0 to 5 days or placed at room temperature (21–25℃) for 0 to 8 h prior to detection. For freeze-thaw stability evaluation, reference samples were subjected to zero to five repeated freeze-thaw cycles. The stability acceptance criteria required that all measured results deviated from the theoretical values by no more than one dilution fold, with no obvious declining trend in detection performance. Robustness was verified by deliberately introducing minor variations to routine experimental parameters. Reference samples at two titer levels—a high titer of 256 and a low titer of 32—were used. For each titer level, aliquots were assigned to four altered conditions: 2 aliquots for 6-h room-temperature placement of detection reagents, 3 aliquots for ±10 min deviation in sample incubation duration, 5 aliquots for ±10 min deviation in antibody incubation duration, and 4 aliquots for a 7-day delay in result collection. Thus, a total of 14 aliquots were tested per titer level under altered conditions (28 aliquots in total across both levels). The assay was considered robust if all test results differed from the theoretical values by no more than one dilution fold. Method consistency study Pre-vaccination and post-vaccination sera were collected from a phase III clinical trial (ChiCTR20252239) of varicella/herpes zoster vaccines. A total of 380 pre-vaccination serum samples were collected from 380 individual participants prior to vaccination, and 610 post-vaccination serum samples were collected from 610 individual participants at 42 days post-vaccination. It should be noted that the pre- and post-vaccination samples were not from fully matched donors, and the sample numbers are reported as total independent samples. Parallel detections were performed using the higher-throughput FAMA method (Optimization of clinical sample detection method) and the FAMA slide assay (FAMA slide assay). Spearman correlation analysis was performed on the detection data using Minitab Statistical Software 21 (Minitab, LLC, PA, USA), and the negative, positive, and overall concordance rates were calculated to evaluate the consistency between the two methods.
