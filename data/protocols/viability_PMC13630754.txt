## Screening procedure
Screening procedure Testing the function of anti-human ENTPD2 antibodies in biochemical system In the initial screening of the biochemical system, in order not to miss any positive antibodies, for the candidate antibody supernatants of unknown concentrations, a repeat screening can be conducted using 5 μL and 15 μL doses. However, the positive antibody mAb1 and the negative antibody mAb13 used a single working concentration and remained constant throughout. The purity of mAb1 and mAb13 antibodies was over 95%. 1) Prepare the reagents. The B-cell culture medium was heated and inactivated at 60 °C in a water bath for 45 minutes, which was denoted as HI (heat inactivation). If DMEM high glucose medium (Servicebio, G4511) (add 10% foetal bovine serum, FBS, gibco, 16000044) is used, the same method should be adopted for heat inactivation; PBS (phosphate buffer saline) (Servicebio, G4202) does not need to be inactivated. (All the PBS used in this study were free of Ca 2+ and Mg 2+ ). Dilute the human ENTPD2 protein with PBS to a concentration of 1.667 ng/μL. Place it on ice. Dilute as needed for use. Dilute the positive control antibody mAb1 (human anti-human ENTPD2, IgG type, patent WO2019/229658A1) in B cell culture medium (HI) (reference materials and methods) to 2 ng/μL (or in HI-DMEM, PBS). Place it on the ice. Dilute the negative control antibody mAb13 (mouse anti-mouse ENTPD2, IgG type, patent WO2019/229658A1) in B cell culture medium (HI) to 2 ng/μL (or in HI-DMEM, PBS). Place it on the ice. Dilute ATP (aladdin, A265857) with PBS to a working solution of 333 nM. Place it on the ice. 2) In 96-well microplate, add ENTPD2 protein, PBS, mAb1, mAb13 antibody, the candidate antibody, and solvent ( Table 1 ). ## Materials and methods
Materials and methods Antibody solvent B cell culture medium: RPMI 1640 (Procell system, PM150140) (containing 2 mM L-glutamine, 10 mM HEPES, 1 mM sodium pyruvate) was supplemented with 5% FBS (gibco, 16000044), 55 µM β-mercaptoethanol (aladdin, M301574), 1% MEM non-essential amino acids (Procell system, PB180424 ), IL-2 (50 ng/mL) (R&D Systems, 6994-R2), IL-4 (10 ng/mL) (R&D Systems, 6939-RB), IL-21 (10 ng/mL) (R&D Systems, 7274-RB) and B-cell activating factor (BAFF) (10 ng/mL) (AmyJet, BGT-PCT-156). All the concentrations mentioned above were the final concentrations. Note: The B-cell culture medium can also be replaced by PBS or DMEM medium (Servicebio, G4511) (containing 10% FBS and without Penicillin/Streptomycin) to reduce costs. The experimental results showed that the detection window in the systems using PBS or DMEM as substitutes was slightly narrower, but this did not affect the evaluation of the results. Antibody and enzyme Candidate antibodies: After immunizing rabbit with human ENTPD2 protein (R&D Systems, 6087-EN), 214 B-cell monoclonal cell culture supernatants (containing IgG-type antibodies) were obtained. The same batch of tests used the same dosage, ranging from 5 μL to 15 μL. To reduce the dosage, make up the volume with PBS. As shown in Table 1 , 15 μL of the supernatant from each antibody was used. If it was reduced to 5 μL (including the control antibody), then 10 μL of PBS was added to the system. ATP hydrolase: the biochemical system used the same ENTPD2 protein (R&D Systems, 6087-EN). Reagents and detection plates CellCounting-Lite ® 2.0 Luminescent Cell Viability Assay (CCL2.0) (Vazyme, DD1101). CCL2.0 is a cell viability detection reagent based on the luciferase system. It contains high purity luciferin and thermostable luciferase. Luciferase consumes luciferin and ATP to emit stable, glow type fluorescence. The light intensity has a linear relationship with the ATP concentration within a certain range. Therefore, this reagent can be used for quantitative detection of the number of living cells. In this study, we directly quantitatively determined the ATP concentration in the solution using the CCL2.0 reagent. Determine the linear range by adjusting the concentration of reagents in the system, and analyze ATP content in different samples. 96 well plate, without tissue culture treatment (NEST, 701012). 96-well ELISA detection plate, black flat bottom (corning, 3916). Cell Cells with overexpressed ENTPD2 (I). Cells constructed through genetic modification based on mouse melanoma cells B16F10. a) Knock out the Entpd2 gene in the wild-type B16F10 cell, and it was recorded as B16F10 mEN KO. b) The human ENTPD2 gene was overexpressed in B16F10 mEN KO cell, and it was recorded as B16F10 hEN OE. When using B16F10 hEN OE as the experimental group, the negative control cell was B16F10 mEN KO. Wild type (WT) B16F10 cells express murine ENTPD2 protein, which also degrades ATP and will interfere with the detection system. We knocked out the mouse ENTPD2 and overexpressed the human ENTPD2. Human ENTPD2 can bind to anti-human ENTPD2 protein, which is consistent with the detection purpose. We used CRISPR/Cas9 to knockout the mouse ENTPD2 and employed a lentiviral delivery system to overexpress the human ENTPD2. Refer to the supplementary file “Construct B16F10 hEN OE cell”. Cells with overexpressed ENTPD2 (II). The human ENTPD2 gene was overexpressed in human embryonic kidney cells HEK293, which was designated as HEK293 hEN OE. When using HEK293 hEN OE as the experimental group, the negative control cells were wild-type HEK293. The overexpression tool was the same as that used for B16F10 hEN OE. Establish mouse tumor model We established syngeneic transplantation model using subcutaneous inoculation. The function of anti-ENTPD2 antibodies in inhibiting tumors requires the involvement of the immune system. The B16F10 melanoma cells originate from C57BL/6 mice, which are immunocompetent mice. Transplanting B16F10 cells into C57BL/6 mice avoids immune rejection. Mouse: C57BL/6J, ♀, at the age of 7 to 8 weeks when inoculating cells. Cell: B16F10 hEN OE. Cell culture medium: DMEM (Servicebio, G4511) (glucose 4500 mg/L) + 10% FBS (gibco, 16000-044) + 1% Penicillin/Streptomycin (Servicebio, G4003). Add puromycin (YEASEN, 60210ES25) with final concentration of 5 μg/mL (for screening purposes). Procedure: 1) Day0, preoperative skin preparation. After the quarantine period, transfer the mice to the workbench. For right-handed individuals, grasp the mouse by gently pinching the skin at the back of its neck with the thumb and index finger of the left hand. Press the hind limbs and tail of the mouse onto the palm using the left ring finger to secure it in place. Expose the right ribcage of the mouse. Hold the trimmer in your right hand and shave from the right side of the abdomen up to the axilla. Approximately 3 cm × 1 cm in length and width, with the long axis extending from the right abdomen to the axilla. Apply a small amount of hair removal cream (brand, Veet) to this haircut area using a cotton swab, then rinse thoroughly with warm water after about 2 minutes. Use sterile paper to absorb the moisture from the skin surface. Place the mouse back into the cage for continued housing. 2) Day 1, prepare cells. For example, using a T25 culture flask, collect the cells when they reach 80% confluence. After removing the medium, wash the cells with 5 mL PBS and then remove the PBS. Add 1 mL of 0.25% trypsin-EDTA (ThermerFisher, 25200056), enough to cover the cells. Digest for approximately 2 minutes at room temperature, and stop the digestion when cells shrink and round up under the microscope. Remove trypsin, add 5 mL of medium containing 10% FBS, and gently tap the edges of the culture flask to detach the cells. Transfer the cell suspension to a centrifuge tube and centrifuge at 4 °C, 300g for 3 minutes to remove the culture medium. Resuspend the cells in 5 mL of cold PBS. Take 18 μL of cell suspension and add 2 μL of Trypan Blue reagent, then mix thoroughly. Transfer 10 μL of the stained sample to the counting chamber of a hemocytometer and count the colorless live cells. If the total number of cells in the large square of the counting chamber exceeds 300, dilute the sample with PBS and recount. Centrifuge to remove PBS, then resuspend the cells in cold PBS according to the count to a concentration of 1×10 7 cells/mL. Place the cells on crushed ice and immediately transfer them to the mouse workbench. Note: It is very important to maintain high cell viability. Improper procedures such as excessive digestion or mechanical damage can affect cell viability, thereby influencing tumor growth. 3) Subcutaneous injection. Gently swirl to mix the cells. Use a 1 mL syringe to draw up 50 μL of suspension (containing 5×10 5 cells). Wipe the needle with an alcohol swab to remove residual cells, preventing them from remaining in the insertion channel and causing irregular tumor shape. Hold the mouse with the left hand as during skin preparation, and use an alcohol swab in the right hand to disinfect the injection site. Wait approximately 10 seconds for the alcohol to evaporate before beginning the injection. Hold the syringe in your right hand with the needle bevel facing upward, and insert it into skin at the lower part of the hairless area. Insert the needle at an angle of approximately 30° into the skin, entering the subcutaneous space between the skin and muscle, without piercing into the underlying muscle. The force feedback from the syringe can be used to determine whether the needle has penetrated the skin; once penetration is detected stop puncturing immediately. Then adjust the syringe so that it is parallel to the skin surface, and use your right thumb and index finger to grasp the outer side of the syringe, advancing it forward. Gently lift the needle tip upward while advancing to avoid puncturing the muscle. Advance the needle approximately 1.0-1.5 cm, then stop. Hold the syringe steady with the right index and middle fingers, and gently push the plunger with the thumb to inject the cell suspension into the subcutaneous space. A circular bulge will form here. Retract the needle. Use your right thumb and index finger to grasp the outside of the syringe and pull it out. While withdrawing the needle, rotate the syringe counterclockwise to prevent the cell suspension from leaking along the needle track. Press the needle site with a dry cotton swab to prevent leakage. Once the needle puncture site has contracted and stabilized, return the mouse to its cage. 4) Observation and measurement. Observe the mice daily. The cells will form a lump in about four days. Measure tumor volume using caliper. Formula for calculating the tumor volume of mice: V = (L×W 2 )/2 V: tumor volume (mm 3 ) L: major axis, the longest diameter (mm) W: minor axis, the diameter perpendicular to L (mm) By day 7, the tumor volume will reach 30-50 mm 3 . Start injecting the antibodies. Mouse in vivo treatment trial Melt the frozen antibodies on ice. Dilute the antibody to 2 μg/μL using cold PBS, place it on ice and transfer it to the animal room. Human-derived anti-human ENTPD2 antibody (positive control mAb1) and rabbit-derived anti-human ENTPD2 antibody (candidate antibody), at a dose of 10 mg/kg, administered by tail vein injection. Mouse-derived anti-mouse PD1 antibody (abinvivo, B176401), at a dose of 10 mg/kg, administered by intraperitoneal injection. In the control group, PBS was used instead of the antibody, with the same volume. Inject the antibody at the time indicated in Table 5 . Inject anti-ENTPD2 antibody once. Two injections of anti-PD1 antibody were administered with a 3-day interval in between. Before starting the injection of the antibodies, the initial body weight and tumor volume of the mice were measured. Monitor body weight and tumor volume every day. Approximately 9-10 days after the start of antibody injection, the experimental endpoint was reached and all the mice were euthanized.
