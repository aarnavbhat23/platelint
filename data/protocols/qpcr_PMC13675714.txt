## Step-by-step method details
Step-by-step method details Generation of Toscana virus (TOSV) working stocks Timing: ∼4–5 days This step describes the amplification of TOSV (strain 1812) 4 in Baby Hamster Kidney-21 (BHK-21) cells and preparation of clarified, titrated working stocks for in vivo infection. Proper stock preparation and aliquoting are essential to maintain viral infectivity and ensure reproducible inoculation across experiments. 1. Seed BHK-21 cells in a T75 to reach 80–90% confluency the following day. 2. Remove culture medium and inoculate cells with TOSV seed stock diluted in phosphate-buffered saline containing 0.75% (w/v) bovine serum albumin (PBSA) at recommended multiplicity of infection (MOI) of 0.5. a. Calculate the MOI based on the cell density at the time of infection. b. Ensure that the inoculum volume evenly covers the cell monolayer in the T75 flask. Note: If seed stock availability is limited, a lower MOI (e.g., 0.1) may also be used. 3. Incubate for 1 h at 37°C to allow viral adsorption. CRITICAL: Gently swirl flask every 20 min during the incubation to ensure uniform infection of the monolayer. 4. After adsorption, add 10 mL of complete DMEM to each T75 flask. a. Return cells to the incubator. Note: Complete DMEM is Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) supplemented with 5% fetal calf serum (FCS), 1% tryptose phosphate broth (TPB), 1% GlutaMAX and 1% penicillin/streptomycin. 5. Monitor daily for cytopathic effect (CPE), which in BHK-21 cells is characterized by cell rounding, increased refractility, detachment from the monolayer and loss of normal cell morphology. CPE typically develops within 3-4 days. 6. Once 70–80% of monolayer displays CPE, collect the culture supernatant (including detached cells). 7. Clarify by centrifugation (1000 × g , 10 min, 4°C) to remove debris. 8. Aliquot clarified supernatant into small volumes, e.g., 250 μL, in sterile cryovials. 9. Store at −80°C (short-term storage) or liquid nitrogen (long-term storage). Pause point: Titrated virus stocks can be stored at −80°C, or in liquid nitrogen for long-term storage, until required. CRITICAL: Prepare small-volume aliquots to avoid repeated freeze-thaw cycles, which significantly reduce viral infectivity and introduce variability between experiments. Plaque assay-based titration of TOSV stocks Timing: ∼5–7 days This step quantifies infectious TOSV in viral stocks or mouse serum samples using a plaque assay on BHK-21 cells. Accurate titration is essential to define the inoculum dose for reproducible in vivo infection experiments. 10. Seed BHK-21 cells in 12 well plates to reach 80% confluency the following day. 11. Prepare 10-fold serial dilutions of TOSV in 0.75% PBSA (typically 10 −1 to 10 −8 ), ensuring sufficient volume, e.g., 500 μL, for at least duplicate wells per dilution. CRITICAL: Keep all virus dilutions on ice and use fresh pipette tips at each dilution step to prevent cross-contamination. 12. Remove culture medium and infect cells with 200 μL of each dilution. 13. Incubate for 1 h at 37°C to allow adsorption. CRITICAL: Gently swirl plate every 20 min to ensure even viral distribution across the monolayer. 14. Overlay cells with 2 mL of a 1:1 mixture of 2xMEM and 1.2% Avicel. 15. Incubate the plates undisturbed for 3 days at 37°C. CRITICAL: Do not move plates during incubation, as disturbance can disrupt plaque formation. 16. Remove overlay and fix cells with 10% PFA (30 min, 20–25°C). 17. Stain cells with 1% crystal violet (30 min, 20°C–25°C). 18. Rinse the wells gently with tap water to remove excess dye and allow the plate to air-dry. 19. Count plaques to determine the titer in plaque-forming units (PFU) ( Figures 1 A and 1B). Figure 1 Representative plaque assay results and calculated viral titres Representative plaque assay results for TOSV strain 1812 (A) and TOSV-mCherry (B) following serial dilution on BHK-21 cells. Viral titres were calculated using the formula: PFU/mL = (average plaque count x dilution factor)/volume plated (mL). Calculated titres were 4 x 10 7 PFU/mL for TOSV strain 1812 and 1.75 x 10 6 PFU/mL for TOSV-mCherry. Note: If the assay is unsuccessful, see troubleshooting 1 . Preparation of TOSV inoculum for subcutaneous foot skin infection Timing: ∼30 min This step describes preparation of TOSV inoculum for subcutaneous infection of Ifnar1 −/− mice, either alone or mixed with SGE. Accurate dilution and handling of small volumes are essential for consistent delivery of defined infectious doses. 20. Retrieve a frozen aliquot of titrated TOSV working stock and thaw rapidly. Keep on ice thereafter. 21. Dilute the virus in sterile 0.75% PBSA to achieve the desired PFU per injection volume. 22. For SGE experimental groups, prepare a 1:1 mixture of diluted TOSV and SGE and keep on ice until injection. a. Use SGE at 1 gland equivalent per μL, giving a final concentration of 0.5 gland equivalents per μL in the inoculum and delivering 1 gland equivalent per mouse in a 2 μL injection unless otherwise stated. b. Dilute the virus so that the intended dose (e.g., 10 5 PFU) is delivered in the final 2 μL after 1:1 mixing. 23. Dilute virus-only controls 1:1 in 0.75% PBSA so that both groups of mice receive the same dose in the same volume. 24. Transport inoculum to the animal facility on ice. CRITICAL: This protocol uses custom-made 33G (point 4 style) microneedles and a 5μL Hamilton 75 RN glass syringe for subcutaneous inoculation delivery into the upper foot skin. Prepare the virus inoculum in sterile 1.5 mL microcentrifuge tubes that allow easier aspiration of the mixture into the syringe. To ensure reproducible downstream viral RNA quantification and cell recovery, collect the same defined anatomical region from all animals during tissue collection (see Step 44). CRITICAL: Prepare fresh virus-SGE mixtures immediately before inoculation. Avoid repeated freeze-thaw cycles of SGE aliquots. Note: Once the viral stock titers are defined, adjust dilution to achieve the desired infectious dose per mouse. In this study, 10 5 PFU per injection was optimal for TOSV strain 1812 in Ifnar1−/− mice. The effective titre may vary depending on viral strain, route of administration, injection site and mouse genotype; optimization is recommended. Note: Phlebotomus perniciosus (Murcia, Spain) SGE was kindly provided by Petr Volf (Charles University, Czech Republic) in aliquots of 100 glands/100μL or 10 glands/10μL of 0.9% NaCl. Lutzomyia longipalpis SGE were kindly provided by Dr. Matthew Rogers (London School of Hygiene and Tropical Medicine, United Kingdom). Four-day-old, non-blood-fed female sand flies were immobilized on ice prior to salivary gland dissection. Under a stereomicroscope, salivary glands were isolated by removing the head and dissecting the glands into phosphate-buffered saline. Glands were disrupted either by 10 s sonication or by two freeze-thaw cycles, followed by centrifugation at 10,000 × g for 2 min to collect the supernatant. 6 , 7 , 8 A detailed video demonstration of this procedure is available, sand fly salivary glands dissection . The term “SGE” refers to salivary gland extract obtained following gland dissection and disruption, “saliva” to refer to the material naturally deposited during sand fly feeding, and “bite” to indicate the live feeding condition. Note: SGE quality control. For working concentration, e xpress doses as salivary gland equivalents in the final inoculum, accounting for the 1:1 dilution with virus, and record the injected volume for each experiment. For Inter-batch SGE consistency, q uantify total protein in each SGE batch using a NanoDrop spectrophotometer (absorbance at 280 nm) and express content as μg protein per gland equivalent. This provides a rapid relative measure for comparing batches rather than absolute quantification; a colorimetric assay (e.g. BCA or Bradford) against a BSA standard curve can be used where absolute values are required. Batches differing by more than ∼20% should not be combined within a study. Comparing batch protein profiles by SDS-PAGE can provide an additional qualitative identity check. For storage stability, store SGE at −70°C in single-use aliquots sized for one experiment, for up to 24 months. Thaw once, keep on ice and do not refreeze; no more than one freeze-thaw cycle is recommended. Where possible, generate single large batches so that all animals within an experiment receive SGE from the same preparation. Species. SGE from P. perniciosus and Lu. longipalpis are not interchangeable: salivary protein composition differs between species and the magnitude of enhancement differs accordingly. Use a single species within an experiment and state the species and dose in figure legends. Optional: Controlled sand fly blood-feeding on upper foot skin prior to TOSV inoculation Timing: ∼30 min per mouse This step describes controlled exposure of Ifnar1 −/− mice to female sand flies to allow blood feeding on the upper foot skin prior to virus infection. This can be used to investigate vector-mediated transmission and assess the saliva-driven modulation of TOSV infection. 25. Prepare female sand flies (∼1 week old). a. Starve the flies by removing sugar 16 to 18 h prior to feeding to promote blood meal uptake. 26. Prepare a custom ventilated feeding chamber that protects the mouse body parts whilst leaving the upper foot skin exposed ( Figure 2 A). Figure 2 Experimental setup for controlled sand fly blood-feeding on mouse upper foot skin (A) A specially designed box that ensures adequate airflow for mice during blood feeding. Note one end of the box, not shown, is open to allow airflow. (B) Two female sand flies were feeding on the upper foot skin of a mouse . 27. Anesthetize mice with 0.1 mL/10 g of Sedator®/Ketavet via intraperitoneal injection. 28. Place the mouse into the sand fly cage and allow 2–4 female sandflies per foot to blood-feed. a. Allow sandflies to feed until fully engorged ( Figure 2 B). 29. Immediately following confirmed feeding, inject the virus subcutaneously into the exposed bitten area of the dorsal foot skin. CRITICAL: Ensure consistent number of bites per mouse to reduce variability. CRITICAL: Cover the toes with sterile tape to prevent unintended biting of distal digits. Note: Blood feeding is restricted to a small, defined area of the upper foot skin; proximity to the bite sites is sufficient to produce local saliva-mediated effects. Note: It is recommended that male sand flies be introduced into the cages at a male-to-female ratio of 1:2 to stimulate biting behaviors in females. Note: Individual bite sites are not reliably visible at this early time point, as feeding does not leave an identifiable mark within the minutes between blood-feeding and inoculation. Reproducibility is instead achieved by restricting the area available for feeding: the ventilated chamber and taping of the toes expose only a small, defined region of dorsal upper foot skin, so all bites are confined to that region. Feeding is confirmed by engorgement of the flies ( Figure 2 B) rather than by inspection of the skin. The virus is injected centrally within this exposed region and is therefore delivered into sand fly-bitten skin, but is not aimed at an individual bite. Given the small size of the exposed area relative to the spread of the subcutaneous bleb, proximity within the bitten region is sufficient and exact superimposition on a single bite is neither achievable nor required. Subcutaneous infection of Ifnar1 −/− mice via upper foot skin inoculation Timing: ∼10–15 min per mouse This step describes inhalation anesthesia and subcutaneous inoculation of Ifnar1 −/− mice with TOSV, either alone, combined with SGE, or following sand fly exposure, into the dorsal upper foot skin (not the footpad). Precise anatomical placement and controlled anesthesia are critical to ensure reproducible local infection and downstream skin-specific analyses. 30. Ensure that all infection materials (virus inoculum, syringes, disinfectants, wipes and related tools) are fully prepared in the biosafety cabinet before anesthetizing the animals. 31. Induce anesthesia using isoflurane (4-5% for induction, ∼1.5–2 L/min oxygen flow). 32. Monitor mice closely. Once fully anesthetized (loss of righting reflex, slow and regular but deep breathing), reduce isoflurane concentration to 3% for maintenance. 33. Remove mice individually and proceed immediately to inoculation. CRITICAL: This protocol uses inhalation anesthesia because it allows rapid induction and recovery, minimizing stress and enabling quick handling. However, prolonged exposure to ≥5% isoflurane can be fatal. Continuously monitor respiratory rate and reflexes throughout the procedure. Note: For experiments involving sand fly biting, mice were anesthetized separately using injectable anesthesia as described in the above section. 34. Place the anesthetized mouse in a prone position with the dorsal side facing upward. 35. Gently extend the hind limb to expose the upper foot skin, preferably left side (not the footpad). 36. Stabilize the foot without overstretching the skin. 37. Load the prepared inoculum into a 5 μL Hamilton syringe. 38. Insert the needle bevel-up at approximately 45° into the upper foot skin to achieve subcutaneous (not intradermal or intramuscular) delivery ( Figure 3 A). Figure 3 Subcutaneous inoculation and standardized skin tissue collection from the upper foot skin (A) Schematic of subcutaneous TOSV inoculation into the upper foot skin using a 45° needle angle. (B) Schematic illustrating excision of the entire upper foot skin surrounding the inoculation site for downstream analyses. Created with BioRender.com . 39. Slowly inject the defined volume (e.g., 2 μL). A small visible bleb at the injection site confirms correct placement. 40. Withdraw the needle carefully and verify absence of leakage or bleeding. 41. Confirm normal posture, mobility and respiration before returning animals to their home cage. Note: Maintaining a consistent inoculation site (e.g., left foot) across experiments minimizes sampling variability and reduces the risk of harvesting uninfected skin during downstream tissue collection. Note: If leakage occurs, see troubleshooting 2 . Collection of skin and systemic tissues at defined time points post-infection Timing: ∼10–20 min per mouse (depending on number of tissues collected) This step describes the standardized excision of the upper foot inoculation site and optional collection of systemic tissues for downstream analyses such as flow cytometry or RNA extraction. 42. Define sampling time points according to the experimental objective. Note: In this protocol, 72 h post-infection (hpi) was used as the earliest reliable time point for consistent viral RNA detection across tissues. 43. Cull mice in accordance with institutional and governmental animal welfare regulations. 44. Place the mouse in a supine position and disinfect the skin with 70% Ethanol. a. Secure limbs using dissection pins and identify the dorsal foot skin inoculation site (upper surface; not the footpad). b. Using sterile scissors and forceps, excise the entire dorsal (upper side) foot skin surrounding the inoculation site, using consistent anatomical boundaries for all animals to ensure that the complete inoculation site is collected ( Figure 3 B). CRITICAL: Maintain consistent excision size across animals to reduce sampling variability between experimental groups. 45. Transfer the skin sample immediately into chilled complete DMEM or RNA preservation solution depending on downstream application. Pause point: Skin samples placed in RNA preservation solution can be stored at −80°C until RNA extraction. Samples intended for flow cytometry must be processed immediately and cannot be stored. 46. If required, collect additional tissues (e.g., draining lymph nodes, spleen, brain and blood) using standard techniques. a. Keep all samples on ice until further processing. Enzymatic digestion of infected upper foot skin to generate single-cell suspensions Timing: ∼1.5–2 h This step describes the enzymatic dissociation of skin tissue to obtain single-cell suspensions suitable for flow cytometry applications such as the detection of mCherry-TOSV-infected cells and downstream molecular applications (e.g., RNA extraction or RNA-seq). Proper digestion conditions are essential to preserve stromal and immune cell integrity while ensuring efficient tissue dissociation. 47. Immediately after collecting, place each skin sample into individual 1.5 mL microcentrifuge tubes containing 500 μL of ice-cold complete DMEM. 48. Keep all samples on ice until digestion. 49. Prepare one fresh digestion solution (∼1.058 mL; see materials and equipment ) for each skin sample and transfer each sample into a separate 1.5 mL microcentrifuge tube containing the digestion solution. Note: Prepare sufficient digestion mix for all samples plus one extra to compensate for pipetting loss. 50. Using sterile, sharp scissors, chop the skin tissue into very small pieces directly in the digestion solution. 51. Incubate samples at 37°C for 50 min, shaking at 1100–1400 rpm (orbital shaking, not centrifugation) using a thermomixer. a. After incubation, immediately place tubes on ice. b. Add 500 μl of chilled complete DMEM to stop enzymatic activity and preserve cell viability. 52. Pass the suspension through a 70 μm cell strainer placed on a sterile 50 ml conical tube. Rinse the original tube and the cell strainer with chilled complete DMEM to maximize collection of cells. 53. Centrifuge cells at 300 × g for 5 min at 4°C. a. Carefully aspirate the supernatant. 54. Resuspend the cell pellet in an appropriate volume of complete DMEM or appropriate downstream buffer (e.g., FACS buffer or MACS buffer). CRITICAL: Avoid vigorous pipetting, which can shear fragile cells. Note: If cell recovery is low, see troubleshooting 3 . Detection of mCherry-TOSV-infected skin cells by flow cytometry Timing: ∼3 h (excluding acquisition and data analysis) This step describes the detection of TOSV-infected cells in skin-derived single-cell suspensions using a recombinant TOSV expressing the fluorescent reporter mCherry (TOSV-mCherry). 3 This approach enables direct identification of infected cells and simultaneous phenotypic characterization using surface markers. 55. Transfer the entire skin-derived single-cell suspension into a 96 well round-bottom plate. Where cell yields allow, plate equal numbers of cells across experimental groups and record the total number stained per sample. 56. Wash cells twice with FACS buffer (PBS without Ca 2+ /Mg 2+ , 0.5% FBS, 2 mM EDTA) at 300 x g for 5 min at 4°C. 57. Resuspend cells in Fc blocking solution. a. Incubate on ice for 5 min. 58. Add antibody master mix (pre-optimized panel for stromal and immune markers). a. Incubate 45 min at 4°C in the dark. Note: Refer to the materials and equipment section for the antibody panels used in this study. 59. Wash cells twice with PBS (300 x g for 5 min at 4°C). 60. Prepare Dead/Live solution in PBS buffer [Zombie UV (Biolegend) is used in this protocol zombie-uv-fixable-viability-kit ]. a. Add Dead/Live solution to cells and incubate 10 min at 20–25°C in the dark. b. Wash cells twice with FACS buffer (300 x g for 5 min at 4°C). 61. Fix cells in 4% paraformaldehyde for 30 min at 20°C–25°C. a. Wash once and resuspend cells in FACS buffer. b. Store samples at 4°C in the dark until acquisition. Pause point: Fixed, stained samples can be stored at 4°C in the dark for up to 48 h before acquisition. 62. Acquire samples on a flow cytometer equipped with appropriate lasers for mCherry detection (e.g., 561 nm excitation). 63. Apply compensation using appropriate single-stained controls or compensation beads prior to gating. 64. Identify infected cells based on mCherry positivity within the live, single-cell population. CRITICAL: Include the following controls in each experiment: uninfected skin samples (to define background fluorescence reference), fluorescence minus one (FMO) controls, each containing all antibodies in the staining panel except the marker of interest, to enable accurate gate placement and single-stained compensation controls (e.g., compensation-beads ). Note: If cell populations are poorly resolved or compensation is inaccurate, see troubleshooting 4 . Note: If intracellular markers (e.g., vimentin) are included in the antibody panel, perform an additional fixation and permeabilization step prior to staining according to the manufacturer's instructions for intracellular staining buffers flow-cytometry-reagents/fixation-permeabilization-kit . Note: This workflow is optimized for genetically modified TOSV expressing mCherry. For wild-type TOSV strains, detection of infected cells would require intracellular viral antigen staining, which is not covered in this protocol. Note: In this protocol, infected cells are identified based on endogenous mCherry fluorescence, which is expressed exclusively in TOSV-infected cells. This strategy allows direct detection of infected cells in freshly isolated skin suspensions. The recombinant reporter virus TOSV ΔNSs:mCherry was generated from lineage A clinical isolate TOSV (strain 1500590) by replacing the NSs gene with the mCherry reporter gene. 3 Note that this reporter virus lacks NSs, the principal type I IFN antagonist of phleboviruses such as TOSV, and is therefore expected to replicate inefficiently in IFN-competent hosts. Its use in this protocol is restricted to Ifnar1 −/− mice, in which type I IFN signalling is already absent (see limitations ). Note: The antibodies used in the flow cytometry staining panels target extracellular markers, apart from vimentin antibody, which is intracellular. Optional: Magnetic enrichment of skin fibroblasts Timing: ∼1.5–2 h This optional step enriches CD90.2 + fibroblasts from skin-derived single-cell suspensions to enable cell type specific downstream analyses such as viral RNA quantification. Tumour-Associated Fibroblast Isolation Kit (Miltenyi Biotec) enables negative depletion of non-fibroblast populations followed by positive selection of CD90.2 + fibroblasts. All named kits were used according to the manufacturer’s instructions, unless otherwise stated miltenyibiotec/tumor-associated-fibroblast-isolation-kit-mouse . 65. Prepare skin-derived single cell suspensions as described in the enzymatic digestion of infected upper foot skin to generate single-cell suspensions step. a. Centrifuge at 300 × g for 10 min at 4°C. 66. Resuspend cells in MACS buffer (PBS without Ca 2+ /Mg 2+ , 0.5% FBS, 2 mM EDTA). 67. Adjust volume to 90 μL MACS buffer per 1 x 10 7 cells. 68. Add the Non-Tumor-Associated Fibroblast Depletion Cocktail. a. Mix gently and incubate for 15 min at 4°C. b. Wash cells by adding MACS buffer and centrifuge at 300 × g for 10 min at 4°C. c. Resuspend pellet in the buffer. 69. Apply the cell suspension onto an LD column placed in a magnetic separator. 70. Collect the flow-through fraction, which contains enriched fibroblasts (non-target cells retained in column). 71. Label fibroblasts by adding CD90.2 (Thy1.2) MicroBeads (20 μL per 1 x 10 7 cells). a. Incubate for 15 min at 4°C. b. Wash cells with MACS buffer and centrifuge at 300 × g for 10 min at 4°C. c. Resuspend pellet in the buffer. 72. Apply the cell suspension onto an MS column placed in the magnetic separator. 73. Remove the column from the magnet and flush to elute CD90.2 + fibroblasts. 74. Count isolated fibroblasts and assess viability. 75. Validate fibroblast enrichment by flow cytometry using fibroblast markers (e.g., CD90.2). CRITICAL: Always verify fibroblast purity before downstream analyses. Note: If purity or viability is compromised, see troubleshooting 5 . Optional: RNA extraction from skin tissue or skin-derived cells and downstream viral RNA analysis Timing: ∼2–4 h (RNA extraction) + downstream application-dependent This step describes RNA extraction from whole skin tissue collected at the skin inoculation site or from skin-derived cell populations (bulk single-cell suspensions or MACS-enriched fibroblasts) generated in this protocol, followed by downstream applications such as RT-qPCR or RNA sequencing. In this study, RNA extraction was performed using: Zymo Quick-RNA Microprep Kit (Zymo Research) zymoresearch/quick-rna-microprep-kit , Invitrogen PureLink RNA Mini Kit thermofisher/PureLink RNA Mini Kit and Invitrogen PureLink Micro Kit thermofisher/PureLink RNA Micro Scale Kit . All named kits were used according to the manufacturer’s instructions unless otherwise specified. 76. Prepare one of the following sample types: a. Upper foot skin tissue collected at defined time points, b. Bulk skin-derived single-cell suspensions, c. MACS-enriched fibroblasts. 77. For skin-derived cell suspensions and MACS-enriched fibroblasts: a. Centrifuge cells at 300 × g for 5 min at 4°C to pellet cells. b. Carefully discard the supernatant, leaving cell pellets. c. Lyse cell pellets directly in lysis buffer supplemented with β-mercaptoethanol according to kit instructions. d. Homogenize lysates using a QIAshredder column or by vigorous pipetting if the column step is omitted. 78. For skin tissue samples: a. Process tissue immediately for RNA extraction or place tissue in RNA stabilizer solution and store at −80°C until extraction. b. If tissue has been stored in RNA stabilizer solution, remove the tissue from the solution. c. Add 1 mL TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, UK) and two 7 mm stainless-steel beads to skin tissue samples. d. Homogenize using a TissueLyser (50 Hz, 10 min). CRITICAL: Skin tissue is fibrous and difficult to disrupt. Incomplete homogenization significantly reduces RNA yield and quality. 79. For lysates from skin tissue and cell pellets, proceed with column-based RNA purification following kit instructions. Note: Include on-column DNase digestion when RNA is intended for RT-qPCR or RNA sequencing. 80. Measure RNA concentration and purity using a spectrophotometer. CRITICAL: Low-input fibroblast enriched samples should be processed using the RNA purification kits optimized for small cell numbers to maximize RNA recovery (e.g., Zymo Quick-RNA Microprep Kit). Using this approach, fibroblast-enriched samples intended for RNA sequencing yielded RNA concentrations ranging from approximately 330 to 2,900 pg/μL. CRITICAL: No carrier RNA or transfer RNA was added during extraction when samples were intended for RNA-seq to avoid interference with downstream library preparation and transcriptomic analyses. 81. Use extracted RNA immediately or store at −80°C until analysis. Pause point: Extracted RNA can be stored at −80°C until analysis. Note: If RNA quality is poor, see troubleshooting 6 .
