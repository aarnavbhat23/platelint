## Step-by-step method details
Step-by-step method details Slicing procedure Timing: About 10 min for steps 1–3; 30 min for steps 4 and 5 (depending on the size of the resection); and finally 30 min for the last steps 6–10 In this step, we describe how to start the slicing procedure ( Figure 1 A) to ensure reproducible generation of uniform huOLCs from lung resection, which is essential for preserving tissue viability and structural integrity for at least five days. 1. Collect the lung tissue samples immediately following surgical resection (performed at the CHRU of Tours or the HCL of Lyon) and transfer into Hibernate®-A containing primocin 0.1 mg/ml (or Penicillin/streptomycin) or in Celsior solution. 2. Transfer the lung resection into a large petri dish containing slicing medium. Note: Gently rinse the resection to remove blood in the slicing medium. 3. Rinse the tissue by simply placing the resection in the slicing medium without using a pipet or any additional tool. 4. Transfer the lung resection to another petri dish containing enough slicing medium to cover the resection. 5. Carefully remove the pleura using a scalpel to facilitate slicing. Trim the lung resection if necessary. 6. Use a sterile 6-mm biopsy punch fitted with a pipette bulb to generate cylindrical tissue cores from the lung resection. CRITICAL: This step should be performed by two people; one person stabilizes the lung resection using tweezers while the other one extracts the sample with the biopsy punch. 7. Place each “core tissue sample” in a 35-mm petri dish filled with 5 ml slicing medium. Let the core tissue sample gently elongate for up to 5min to ensure a proper form for slicing. 8. Once all the core tissue samples are prepared, transfer one tissue core onto 4 autoclaved Whatman papers, and humidify the core tissue with a drop of slicing medium. 9. Place the Whatman papers onto the slicing platform of the McIlwain tissue chopper. CRITICAL: The tissue chopper blade is extremely sharp. Use caution when placing and adjusting samples. 10. Perform the slicing (500 μm). See Troubleshooting problems 1 and 2 . 11. Transfer the Whatman paper with the slices to a new 35-mm petri dish with 5 ml of fresh slicing medium. 12. Carefully remove the human lung organotypic slices using curved sterile tweezers and separate the lung slices. 13. Remove all the Whatman paper from the petri dish. Note: The number of successful huOLCs per tissue sample will depend on the size of the resection (particularly its thickness) and the anatomical area (more or fewer pleura, dark areas). We can usually obtain between 5 and 10 per biopsy punch of 1 cm thick tissue. More details are presented in Figure 1 B. Transfer onto inserts and culture procedure Timing: About 10 min for steps 14–16 and 20 min for steps 16–20 (variable, depending on the number of slices) These steps describe how to correctly place tissue cultures on the insert to enable the efficient exchange of nutrients and gas at the air-liquid interface, thereby reducing hypoxia and maintaining tissue viability. The plating procedure is essential to ensure uniform exposure to viral inoculum and/or treatment to preserve reproducible infection/treatment efficiency. 14. Inside a safety cabinet carefully remove the medium under the insert using a P1000 Pipetman with filter tips 15. Add 1 mL of fresh medium under the insert before depositing the human organotypic lung slices. 16. Replace the 6 well plate into the incubator under a humidified atmosphere of 5% CO 2 and 37°C. CRITICAL: This must be completed prior to the beginning of the slicing procedure to avoid drying of the insert membrane and to allow immediate plating of generated slices. 17. Select slices for the culture under the stereomicroscope. Note: slices must be uniform in thickness and round in shape without signs of folding or tearing. Depending on the addressed question, avoid using cultures presenting signs of fibrosis or tar deposits. 18. Carefully place between 2 to 6 slices per insert membrane using the 5 mL truncated pipette mounted on a pipette bulb. Note: huOLCs should be placed on the insert as presented in the schematic below without touching each other or the edges of the membrane, as contact with the edges can alter infection patterns and affect the viability of huOLCs ( Figure 3 ). Gently remove the excess of slicing medium on the membrane by using a P200 Pipetman avoiding displacement of slices. 20. Incubate the slices at 37°C, under 5% CO 2 in a humidified atmosphere. See Troubleshooting problems 3 and 4 . Viability procedure Timing: MTT (3-(4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl)-2,5-diphenyltetrazolium bromide) Cell Viability Assay (steps 21a–e), about 5 h (including the incubation time) LDH-Glo Cytotoxicity assay a-d about 30 min including LDH storage buffer preparation and 1 h 10 min for steps e-k. Cells viability live/dead assay a-r about 1h30min until cytometry monitoring. Cells viability in huOLCs by fluorescence microscopy a-e about 2 h until microscopy observation. In this step, we assess tissue viability using complementary methods of (3-(4,5-dimethylthiazol-2-yl)-2,5-diphenyltetrazolium bromide) MTT and Lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) assays, to monitor cellular integrity and metabolic activity. MTT is used as a proxy for tissue viability using quantitative viability assays while LDH quantifies tissue damage by measuring the release of cytoplasmic enzymes into the culture medium. We have completed this study with a flow cytometry method using LIVE/DEAD staining, and a microscopic method using propidium iodide to visualize cell death directly on the tissue counterstained with Hoechst. These last two methods make it possible to evaluate the percentage of cell death among total cells per observed section. 21. MTT Cell Viability Assay (see Figure 4 A) a. Assess the viability of the human lung organotypic slices at 0, 24, 48, and 72 h timepoints. b. The MTT used is a Sigma Aldrich powder (1 g), reconstituted in sterile 1X DPBS under BSC to achieve the final working concentration of 5 mg/ml. c. Transfer one huOLC per well into a 96-well plate and add 70 μL of MTT solution incubate for 4 h at 37°C. Alternatively, prepare a separate plate fully dedicated to viability assay. In this case either immerse slices into MTT solution or directly add 70 μL of MTT solution on the top of each slice to fully cover the huOLC to ensure total exposure of the tissue. CRITICAL: MTT is toxic for the skin and eyes and must be handled in a chemical fume hood and BSL-2/4 conditions are required when working with viruses. Wear gloves and protective eyewear. Note: MTT can be prepared in advance, stored at 4°C protected from light for up to 3 weeks. d. Transfer each slice into a well of a 48-well plate and cover with 200 μL of DMSO, and triturate to solubilize formazan crystals (violet solution). e. After 10 min incubation at 37°C, transfer 75 μL of DMSO containing formazan, without tissue pieces in a transparent 96-well plate to measure absorbance at 540 nm with a microplate reader (e.g., Tristar 5, Berthold). 22. Prepare LDH storage buffer according to manufacturer instructions (see reagents setup section). b. Reconstitute LDH with 275 μL of Storage Buffer and mix to make a 1,000 U/mL standard. The LDH standard is aliquoted at −20°C. c. Collect 1.5 μL of homogenized subnatant into 148.5 μL of LDH storage buffer to preserve LDH. d. Store aliquots at −20°C until day of the assay. The day of LDH quantification. e. Thaw samples, LDH detection enzyme mix, LDH storage buffer and LDH standard at ∼25°C. f. Prepare LDH standard dilutions as follows: i. Dilute 10 μL of LDH standard in 3.115 mL LDH storage Buffer to reach 3.2 U/mL LDH final concentration. ii. Mix 10 μL of 3.2 U/mL LDH standard with 990 μL of LDH storage buffer to reach final concentration of 32 mU/mL. iii. Serially dilute 1:2 the 32 mU/mL LDH standard solution by mixing 200 μL of previous concentration with 200 μL of LDH storage buffer until reaching LDH final concentration of 0.5 mU/mL. g. Prepare LDH detection reagent by mixing LDH Enzyme Buffer Mix and Reductase: for one reaction, mix 50 μL of LDH assay with 0.25 μL of reductase. h. Add 50 μL of sample or standard in a white 96-well plate. i. Add 50 μL of detection reagent buffer per well. j. Incubate the plate at ∼25°Cfor 30 min. k. Measure Luminescence using Tristar 5 reader (e.g., Berthold). 23. Cells viability analysis ( Figure 4 B) a. Infection procedure Timing: About 30 min for steps 37–39 (depending on the number of slices) This section describes the viral infection of huOLCs by Influenza A virus (IAV) and recombinant Nipah virus (Malaysia strain), expressing the gene encoding the EGFP (rNiV-EGFP), under controlled conditions to ensure efficient viral entry and preserving tissue viability ( Figure 7 ). Precise parameters are essential for reproducible downstream analyses. Note: All procedures described in the following protocols are performed in biological safety cabinets under sterile working conditions, while wearing personal protective equipment in accordance with BSL-2 and BSL-4 regulations for IAV and NiV, respectively. Refer to your local regulations and permits to determine what biosafety level is appropriate for your strains. 37. Dilute the viral stock in sterile MEM medium for A/Scotland/20/74 (H3N2) or in sterile Opti-MEM medium for rNiV-EGFP to infect the huOLCs. Note: For both viruses, FBS free medium is used. CRITICAL: Follow the good laboratory practices and personal protective equipment. 38. Infect the huOLCs with 2,000 to 20,000 plaque forming units (pfu) of A/Scotland/20/74 (H3N2) (BSL-2) or 5,000 or 20,000 pfu of rNiV-EGFP (BSL-4). Using low-binding 10 μL pipette tips, dispense a drop of 4 μL of the virus directly on the slice until the collection time point without removing or washing the viral inoculum. Note: For the mock control slices, apply the same volume of MEM or Opti-MEM as for the infection. CRITICAL: Ensure that the virus drop is completely and homogeneously distributed on the slice without damaging the tissue. 39. Incubate the infected slices at 37°C under 5% CO 2 in a humidified atmosphere. 40. For rNiV-EGFP, the fluorescence resulting from viral replication can be followed using a Leica DMIRB microscope, a fluorescence microscope (BSL-4) and Nikon Eclipse Ts2R optical microscope in BSL-2. Aliquot and store huOLC subnatants at −80°C. The day of the assay: 72. Humidify the 96-well PALL® filter plate with 100 μL of wash buffer. 73. Aspirate wash Buffer gently using a vacuum manifold. CRITICAL: Do not touch the filter with tips. Do not exceed 10′ in Hg of vacuum pressure. Aspirate for 2 to 10 s until wells are drained. 74. Preparing Human Flex Set Standards: a. Preparation of the top standard: Mix lyophilized standards from each BD CBA human Soluble Protein Flex Set in a 15 mL falcon tube with 2 mL assay diluent to reach a 5,000 pg/mL dilution. b. Incubate top standard solution for 15 min at ∼25°C. CRITICAL: Do not vortex. c. Dilute standard solution by half by mixing 200 μL of standard with 200 μL assay diluent. d. Repeat the 2-fold dilution process until reaching 1:256 dilution, e.g., 8 serial dilution tubes + 1 top standard. Note: Additional dilutions can be performed if required. e. Prepare a negative control with assay diluent only. f. Distribute 40 μL of standard dilution or negative control per well. g. Dilute subnatants in Assay diluent to remain within the standard range for each cytokine. 75. Mixing Human Soluble Protein Flex Set Capture beads: a. Determine the number of BD CBA human Soluble Protein Flex Sets to be used, as well as the number of samples (including standards) in the experiment. b. Vortex each capture beads stock vial for 15 s to resuspend beads. c. For each set, and as many as sample, mix 0.8 μL of each cytokine capture bead complete to 40 μL with Capture beads diluent. Note: The volumes have to be adapted to the number of samples (S) and cytokines (Y) to be assessed. Use a formula to calculate the total volume V according to the number of samples (S). V= Y x 0.8 μL cytokine capture beads x S. We recommend to count the real number of sample + 1 to ensure a dead volume. d. Distribute 40 μL of the capture beads mix over 40 μL of sample or standard. e. Cover the plate with seal. f. Shake the plate for 2 min at 500 rpm. g. Incubate for 1 h at ∼25°C, in the dark. h. Keep an aliquot of the beads for setup. 76. Preparing Human Soluble Protein Flex Set PE Detection Reagents: a. For each set mix and as many as sample, mix 0.8 μL of each Human Soluble Protein Flex Set PE detection reagent complete to 40 μL with detection reagent diluent. Note: As above V= Y x 0.8μL cytokine capture beads x (S+1). b. Distribute 40 μL of PE detection mix per well. c. Cover the plate with new seal. d. Shake the plate for 2 min at 500 rpm. e. Incubate for 2 h at ∼25°C, in the dark. f. Aspirate on vacuum manifold until wells are drained. g. Add 150 μL per well of wash buffer. h. Aspirate on vacuum manifold until wells are drained. i. Add 200 μL per well of wash buffer. j. Shake the plate for 5 min at 500 × g to resuspend beads. k. Proceed to sample acquisition on flow cytometer (e.g., LSR Fortessa II cytometer, BD Biosciences) according to manufacturer’s instruction (see Troubleshooting Problems 6 and 7 ).
