## Procedure
Procedure Thawing and Plating iPSCs Due to the ~ 18-hour attachment period, we start the procedure in the afternoon (16:00) to allow the first medium change at 10:00. Prepare recombinant human vitronectin-N (VTN)-coated 6-well plates, according to the manufacturer’s recommendations. If using plates stored at 4 °C, allow them to equilibrate at room temperature (RT) for 1 h. Prepare Complete Essential 8 (E8) Medium as described in the supplementary information (“Preparation of cell culture media and buffers”, page 2) and pre-warm it to RT. Add CultureCEPT supplement to the pre-warmed medium. Pre-warm a water bath at 37 °C. Thaw the iPSC cryovial in a water bath at 37 °C by gently swirling and rocking every few seconds until only a small ice crystal remains. Transfer the iPSC suspension to a 15 millilitre (mL) tube using a 10 mL serological pipette. Note: Avoid pipetting up and down, as this may cause mechanical stress and reduce cell viability. 6. Slowly dispense complete E8 medium supplemented with CultureCEPT dropwise to the tube while gently rocking the tube every few seconds, reaching a final volume of 10 mL. 7. Centrifuge the cell suspension at 100 relative centrifugal force (x g) for 3 min at RT. 8. Carefully remove the supernatant, leaving enough residual medium to cover the cell pellet. Gently tap the bottom of the 15 mL tube to dislodge the pellet. 9. Aspirate the VTN-coated plate. 10. Using a 10 mL serological pipette, resuspend the cells by adding 2 mL of complete E8 medium supplemented with CultureCEPT to the pellet and transfer directly to one VTN-coated well. Note: Avoid pipetting up and down to minimise mechanical stress, which can compromise viability. 11. Rock the plate with gentle side‑to‑side and back‑and‑forth motions to evenly distribute the iPSCs. Incubate at 37 °C, 5% CO 2 for 18 h. 12. After 18 h, wash the cells with 2 mL/well Complete E8 medium, aspirate and add 2 mL/well Complete E8 medium. 13. After an additional 24 h, replace the medium daily with 2 mL/well of Complete E8 medium. Monitor the iPSCs daily for growth, density/confluence and morphology. Passaging and Expansion of iPSCs When iPSCs have reached 60–75% confluence, perform EDTA-based clump passaging onto VTN-coated 6-well plates. Prepare 0.5 mM ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) in Dulbecco’s phosphate-buffered saline without calcium and without magnesium (DPBS −/− ) and coat 6-well plates with VTN. Rinse the iPSCs once with 3 mL/well DPBS -/- , then incubate with 1 mL/well 0.5 mM EDTA at 37 °C, 5% CO 2 for 2–3 min. Colonies should still remain attached, but their margins should begin to loosen and appear slightly refractile or translucent. Aspirate the VTN solution from the new 6-well plate. Carefully remove the 0.5 mM EDTA from the iPSC well. For a standard 1:4 split ratio, gently resuspend the cells in approximately 8 mL of Complete E8 medium using a 10 mL serological pipette. Perform a single, gentle circular motion from the centre outward to evenly detach and resuspend the loosened colonies. Note: Perform only once. Do not re-attempt detachment if some colonies remain attached after the first pass. Additional pipetting or agitation can damage the cells that have already detached. Any colonies that remain firmly attached should be discarded. 5. Transfer the cell suspension to the new VTN-coated 6-well plate by dispensing 1 mL/well into 4 separate wells twice to reach a final volume of 2 mL/well. 6. Gently rock the plate side‑to‑side and back‑and‑forth to ensure even cell distribution. Incubate at 37 °C, 5% CO 2 for 24 h. 7. After 24 h, rinse with 2 mL/well Complete E8 medium and feed with 2 mL/well Complete E8 medium. Continue with daily medium changes without rinsing until the cells reach 60–75% confluence and are ready for passaging. Freezing iPSCs The iPSCs should be 60–75% confluent at the time of freezing. We recommend culturing the cells in 6-well plates and resuspending each well in 2 mL freezing medium and aliquoting 1 mL/vial, which typically corresponds to 0.8–1‧10 6 iPSCs per vial when 60–75% confluent. Prepare > 2 mL Complete E8 medium supplemented with 10% DMSO (freezing medium) for each well of iPSC culture to be cryopreserved. Rinse the cells with 3 mL/well DPBS -/- and incubate with 1 mL/well 0.5 mM EDTA at 37 °C, 5% CO 2 for 2–3 min. Aspirate the 0.5 mM EDTA solution and resuspend the iPSCs with 2 mL of freezing medium using a 10 mL serological pipette. Note: Do not attempt to recover colonies left behind after the initial resuspension. The cell suspension should be handled only once, as repeated pipetting can cause substantial loss of cell viability. 4. Transfer the cell suspension into cryovials, dispensing 1 mL/vial. 5. Place the cryovials in a freezing container such as a CoolCell and transfer to a −80 °C freezer for 24-48 h before transferring the vials to < −140 °C for long-term cryostorage. Assemble CRISPR components in nuclease-free PCR strips and use a thermocycler to ensure precise temperature control and stability during critical incubation steps. Refer to the instrument manual for step-by-step instructions and visual guidance. Note: Do not use iPSCs for downstream applications < 72 h post-electroporation. Screening of NHEJ and HDR in CRISPR-Edited iPSC Pools Timing: 2 days. Before you Start Screening for NHEJ editing in CRISPR-edited iPSC pools is a useful preliminary step to identify the suitable gRNA candidates for subsequent HDR experiments (Fig. 3 ). In Roman et al., 2026 [ 29 ], this approach was applied to six candidate gRNAs (Fig. 4 B) to identify the most suitable guide for subsequent HDR editing (Fig. 4 C). Importantly, the NHEJ screening experiment is performed in the absence of a donor DNA template to assess guide-dependent cleavage activity independently of HDR. A minimum of five candidate sgRNAs is recommended for reliable comparison and guide selection. Therefore, NHEJ screening is performed on CRISPR-edited iPSC pools that are treated as terminal samples and gDNA is isolated from a conventional cell pellet. By contrast, early HDR analysis can be performed on recovering electroporated iPSC pools simultaneously across multiple experimental conditions at once while the cells remain in culture, enabling only selected pools to be advanced for expansion and downstream single-cell cloning. Because a conventional cell pellet is unavailable until a pool has been passaged, gDNA can be prepared from cellular material recovered from the culture supernatant at least 72 h after electroporation. If needed, two consecutive supernatant collections can be pooled to reach a starting volume of 1 mL. Screening and Expansion of the Clonal iPSCs Timing: 1 week. Before Proceeding Perform Sanger sequencing during the expansion phase in 24-well plates to selectively expand only the desired clonal iPSC lines. This targeted approach helps optimise time and resources by avoiding the need to freeze and store large numbers of low-quality or undesired clones. Instead of freezing the entire clonal population prematurely, we recommend expanding only selected, sequence-verified clones and generating larger, high-quality cryopreserved batches after confirmation. This reduces unnecessary freeze-thaw cycles and preserves the long-term viability and function of the best-performing iPSC clones. Procedure Collect the cell supernatant and centrifuge at 500 x g for 5 min. Carefully aspirate the supernatant and resuspend the pellet in 20 µL dilution buffer and 0.5 µL DNA Release Additive (Phire Animal Tissue Direct PCR Kit). Mix well by briefly vortexing and spin down. Note: If a clear pellet is not visible, leave 30 µL residual supernatant to reduce the risk of sample loss. However, this will result in lower final DNA concentration. 3. Incubate the tubes for 2–5 min at room temperature, then immediately transfer to the pre-warmed heat block at 98 °C for 2 min. 4. Briefly spin down the tubes to collect condensation and proceed directly with PCR amplification and gel electrophoresis, using the primers and conditions optimised in the “Screening of NHEJ and HDR in CRISPR-edited iPSC pools” chapter “Sanger sequencing”. 5. Note: Before proceeding with advanced genome-wide analyses such as WES or whole-genome sequencing (WGS) and selecting the clonal iPSC line for downstream experiments, we recommend using the functional integrity and differentiation potential of the edited clonal iPSC line as eligibility criteria. In our workflow, directed organoid differentiation is performed to compare differentiation efficiency across candidate clones before selecting the final line. Whole-exome sequencing (WES) of the selected clone was subsequently performed to confirm clonality and to rule out off-target events.
