## Materials and equipment
Materials and equipment CRITICAL: Trizol reagent contains toxic and corrosive components. Avoid skin, eye and respiratory contact. Perform all operations in a fume hood with nitrile gloves and safety goggles. CRITICAL: Chloroform is volatile, toxic and irritant. It may damage respiratory tract and eyes. Always use in a well-ventilated fume hood and wear protective gloves and goggles.

## Step-by-step method details
Step-by-step method details Tomato seedling preparation and wounding treatment Timing: 3 weeks This section covers standardized tomato seed germination, seedling cultivation and uniform mechanical leaf wounding. Consistent seedling age and identical wounding intensity are critical to minimize variation in wound-induced defense gene expression. 1. Tomato seed germination: a. Select culture dishes with 9 cm diameter. b. Place the seed germination paper at the bottom of the culture dish. c. Surface-sterilize tomato seeds prior to germination to minimize microbial contamination. i. Rinse seeds with 75% ethanol for 2 min. ii. Incubate seeds in 5% sodium hypochlorite solution for 10 min with gentle shaking. iii. Wash seeds 6–7 times with sterile distilled water to remove residual disinfectant. d. Spread the tomato seeds evenly on the germination paper. e. Add distilled water to the culture dish to fully moisten the germination paper. Ensure seeds are in full contact with water, but not submerged under the water. f. Culture the seeds in the dark for 2 to 3 days until the radicle just begins to emerge. Note: Germination paper is an ISTA compliant, chemically inert, highly absorbent cellulose blotting paper designed for seed germination assays, free of bleaching agents or toxic additives that may disturb seed physiology or subsequent gene-expression measurements. CRITICAL: Surface sterilization is required to minimize microbial contamination. Without sterilization, fungal or bacterial contamination may occur during germination, which can induce plant defense responses and alter defense-related gene expression, confounding downstream qPCR analysis. CRITICAL: Sow germinated seeds before cotyledons turn fully green. 16 cm straight tweezers are used to sow germinated seeds. Otherwise, when the seeds are sown in the tray, they are likely to be subjected to stress. 2. Seedling transplantation and cultivation: a. Fill small square plastic pots (7 cm side length; round pots of equivalent volume are also acceptable) with the prepared growing substrate. b. Move the tomato seeds whose radicles have broken through the seed coat into the seedling tray. c. Wait for the tomato seedlings to grow into a two leaf, one heart state ( Figure 1 ). Note: The substrate is prepared by mixing vermiculite and peat in a 1:1 volume ratio. CRITICAL: During the growth period of tomato seedlings, it is necessary to control the water supply to avoid stress caused by excessive watering. Grow seedlings in a growth chamber under controlled conditions (16 h of light at 26°C and 8 h of dark at 18°C with 60% relative humidity, light intensity: 180–220 μmol m −2 s −1 ). No additional fertilizer is required at this stage, since the substrate provides adequate nutrients. 3. Mechanical wounding treatment of tomato leaves: a. Select healthy tomato seedlings at the two leaf, one heart stage. b. Sterilize 14 cm straight hemostatic forceps with 75% ethanol and air dry completely before use. c. Use 14 cm straight hemostatic forceps to wound the lamina of the first true leaf by one single clamp with consistent pressure; this treatment produces visible leaf drooping ( Figure 2 ). d. Number and label treated seedlings, and set up an untreated control group consisting of independent seedlings grown in separate pots under identical growth conditions. e. Harvest three separate samples at 12 h post-treatment, followed by rapid snap-freezing in liquid nitrogen: i. The wounded leaflet of the first true leaf (only the wounded leaflet, not the whole trifoliate leaf, is harvested for this local sample). ii. The undamaged second true leaf from the same seedling for systemic response analysis. iii. Undamaged leaves from independent untreated seedlings (control). Note: Ensure the wounded area on the leaf blade faces vertically downward after mechanical wounding. Note: Separate sampling should be performed for mechanically wounded leaves and undamaged second true leaves (systemic) collected from the same wounded seedlings, as well as untreated control leaves harvested from independent untreated tomato seedlings (control). CRITICAL: Sterilize the hemostatic forceps between every individual seedling and between different genotypes to avoid cross-contamination of plant sap. 4. Leaf sample storage: a. Place leaves frozen in liquid N2 directly into a −80°C freezer right after collection. b. Clearly label each sample for tissue type (control, wounded, systemic leaf), treatment time (12 h after wounding) and sample serial number. CRITICAL: Repeated freezing and thawing events will degrade RNA and other biomolecules; minimize changes in storage temperature for stored samples. CRITICAL: Perform RNA extraction within 3 months after sampling. Long-term storage may cause RNA degradation and compromise the reliability of qPCR results. Total RNA extraction from tomato leaves Timing: 2–3 h This step details total RNA isolation from control, wounded and systemic tomato leaf tissue via column-based purification. Intact, high-purity RNA is essential to obtain reliable cDNA for subsequent quantitative transcription analysis. 5. Grind leaf samples: a. Retrieve frozen leaf tissue from the −80°C freezer and put this into a cold mortar. b. Continue adding liquid nitrogen while mortar grinding to ensure tissue is completely frozen. c. Mix powdered tissue to a pre-cooled 1.5 mL centrifuge tube, with the amount of powdered tissue up to the 500 μL line. Note: Grind tissue completely to fine powder without any visible leaf pieces. Not enough grinding results in a lower RNA final quality. Note: The powdered ground tissue can be divided into two tubes. One tube will be held in as a reserve. Note: Ground leaf powder must remain completely frozen throughout the procedure. Do not allow the tissue to thaw at any point before adding Trizol reagent, as thawing will cause RNA degradation. 6. RNA Extraction: a. Add 1 mL Trizol reagent to the tissue powder, mix well, and leave the mixture at room temperature for 5 min. b. Transfer the mixed solution into the adsorption column placed in the collection tube in two portions. Centrifuge at 4°C and 13500 × g for 20 s, then discard the solution in the collection tube. c. Add 700 μL of Buffer RW1 to the adsorption column, centrifuge at 4°C at 13500 × g for 20 s, and discard the solution in the collection tube. d. Add 500 μL of Buffer RW2 reagent to the adsorption column. Centrifuge at 4°C for 13500 × g for 20 s. Discard the solution in the collection tube. Repeat this step once. e. Centrifuge at 13500 × g for 2 min at 4°C, then let the adsorption column dry completely. f. Place the adsorption column in a new 1.5 mL RNase-free centrifuge tube. Add 30 μL of RNase-free water to the center of the column and let it stand for 1 min. g. Centrifuge at 13500 × g for 1 min at 4°C to collect RNA solution, then quantify RNA concentration via spectrophotometry. h. Detect RNA concentration and purity via NanoDrop spectrophotometer ( Figure 3 ). i. Validate RNA integrity by agarose gel electrophoresis before reverse transcription. j. Finally, store the RNA in a −80°C refrigerator. Note: The extracted RNA samples should be avoided from undergoing repeated freezing and thawing, as this will adversely affect the quality of the RNA. Note: Alternative column-based RNA extraction kits are also compatible, as long as they can produce intact and high-purity total RNA from plant leaf tissue. Figure 3 Quality and concentration detection of total RNA extracted from tomato leaves RNA quality and concentration were analyzed using a spectrophotometer. The absorbance at 260 nm was measured for concentration calculation, while the A260/A280 and A260/A230 ratios were used to assess RNA purity. Reverse transcription for cDNA synthesis Timing: 1 h Here we outline DNase treatment and first-strand cDNA synthesis from purified leaf RNA. Complete genomic DNA removal ensures accurate quantification of target wound-responsive transcripts in downstream qPCR. 7. Reverse Transcription: a. Prepare all reaction reagents on ice to avoid RNA degradation and reagent inactivation ( Figure 4 ). b. Add the following reagents to a sterile, RNase-free centrifuge tube in sequence: Reagent Amount 2 μl 5X gDNA Eraser Buffer 2 μL gDNA Eraser 1 μL total RNA 1 μg ddH 2 O Adjust to 10 μL c. Incubate at 42°C for 2 min, then place the tube on ice temporarily. d. Add 10 μL of RT master mix into the above 10 μL gDNA-removed solution, mix gently and spin: Reagent Amount 5×PrimeScript Buffer 2 4 μL PrimeScript RT Enzyme Mix I 1 μL RT Primer Mix 1 μL ddH 2 O 4 μL e. Incubate at 37°C for 15 min, followed by 85°C for 5 s to terminate the reaction. Store cDNA on ice or at −80°C for long-term preservation. Note: Genomic DNA is eliminated via 2 min incubation at 42°C using gDNA Eraser possessing potent DNA-degrading activity. Note: Alternative cDNA synthesis kits can also be used according to researchers’ preference, provided they can generate high-quality first-strand cDNA suitable for subsequent qPCR analysis. Note: When multiple reactions need to be carried out simultaneously, the mixed solutions of various reagents (Master Mix; which include RNase Free ddH 2 O, Buffer, enzymes, etc.) should be prepared first, and then aliquoted into each reaction tube. This will ensure a more accurate volume of the reagents taken, reduce the loss of reagents, and avoid repeatedly taking the same reagent. At the same time, it can also minimize the errors caused by experimental operations or between experiments. (Created with BioGDP). Quantitative real-time PCR and data analysis Timing: 2 h This workflow describes SYBR Green-based qPCR setup and statistical normalization of defense gene transcripts. Melting curve validation and statistical testing are required to confirm specific, reproducible gene expression results. 8. Quantitative RT-PCR setup: a. Use the Kapa biosystem for qPCR analysis. The 20 μL reaction system includes: Reagent Amount cDNA 2 μL SYBR Fast qPCR Kit 10 μL RT-F primer (0.25 μM) 0.5 μL RT-R primer (0.25 μM) 0.5 μL ddH 2 O Adjust to 20 μL b. Use the Roche LightCycler 480 real-time PCR system with the following cycling conditions ( Figure 5 ): Steps Temperature Time Cycles Initial Denaturation 95°C 30 s 1 Denaturation 95°C 5 s 40 cycles Annealing 59°C 30 s Note: The annealing temperature should be empirically determined and optimized for each specific primer set. Note: Alternative qPCR platforms, such as TaqMan probe-based assays, may also be used, provided they are validated for the target genes and produce specific, reproducible amplification. CRITICAL: Melt curve analysis is required after qPCR amplification to confirm amplicon specificity. Heat from 60°C to 95°C at a rate of 0.3°C/s, with continuous fluorescence acquisition. 9. Data analysis: a. Normalize the expression levels of the target genes against those of tomato ACTIN2 ( Figure 6 ). b. Select appropriate statistical tests to determine the significance of differences according to the experimental design. Note: Define significance standards: ∗ P <0.05; ∗∗ P <0.01; ∗∗∗ P <0.001; ns means no significant difference. A two-tailed Student’s t -test is used for pairwise comparisons between two groups. For datasets with multiple genotypes, tissue types, or time points, ANOVA followed by post-hoc multiple comparison tests are recommended.
