## 2. Materials and Methods
2. Materials and Methods 2.1. Cell Culture Human foreskin fibroblasts (HFF1, ATCC ® SCRC-1041™) were obtained from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC, Manassas, VA, USA) and cultured under standard conditions using Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle’s Medium (DMEM; Life Technologies, Carlsbad, CA, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS; Life Technologies, Carlsbad, CA, USA), 2 mM L-glutamine (Euroclone, Milan, Italy), and 1% penicillin/streptomycin (Euroclone, Milan, Italy), maintaining controlled environmental conditions of temperature, humidity, and CO 2 levels. Treated and control cells were seeded and maintained in parallel under equivalent conditions. Confluence at the beginning of treatment and passage number were kept comparable across experimental conditions to minimize variability related to growth phase or phenotypic drift. Cells were allowed to reach appropriate confluence prior to treatment to ensure stable proliferative conditions and to limit growth-dependent differences in gene expression. 2.2. REAC Technology and Protocol Architecture REAC technology uses standardized, device-embedded protocols delivered through an Asymmetric Conveyor Probe (ACP). Within the Inside Blue Zone framework, ACT-IBZ (anti-inflammatory cellular treatment), CO-IBZ (circulatory optimization), and MO-IBZ (metabolic optimization) are distinct, operator-independent protocols. They share the BENE mod 110 technological platform and the standardized IBZ session architecture but retain separate device-embedded codifications. These configurations are automatically implemented by the device and cannot be modified by the operator. The present study evaluated the cellular response specifically associated with the CO-IBZ protocol. 2.3. REAC CO-IBZ Treatment Protocol HFF1 cells were treated with the REAC Circulatory Optimization—Inside Blue Zone (CO-IBZ) protocol using a BENE 110 device (ASMED, Scandicci, Italy). CO-IBZ is a preset, device-embedded protocol whose delivery parameters cannot be independently selected or modified by the operator. The treatment cycle comprised nine 30-min sessions administered over three consecutive days, with three sessions performed each day. Each subsequent session began one hour after completion of the preceding session. This temporal organization reproduced the standard clinical administration schedule of the CO-IBZ protocol. During each session, the Asymmetric Conveyor Probe (ACP) was maintained in direct contact with the cell culture for 30 min. The ACP functions as the asymmetric conveyance interface of the REAC device and does not deliver an exogenous electric current to the culture. Untreated control cultures were maintained and handled in parallel under equivalent culture and environmental conditions. 2.4. Gene Expression Analysis Following completion of the treatment cycle, total RNA was extracted from control and treated fibroblasts using the RNeasy Mini Kit (Qiagen, Hilden, Germany). RNA concentration and purity were assessed spectrophotometrically. Quantitative real-time PCR (RT-qPCR) was performed to assess changes in SIRT1, NOX4, VEGF, p16, p21 and HAS2 expression levels using the Luna ® Universal One-Step RT-qPCR Kit (New England Biolabs, Ipswich, MA, USA) on a CFX Thermal Cycler (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA). GAPDH was used as the reference gene. Relative expression was calculated using the 2 −ΔΔCt method, assuming comparable amplification efficiencies between target and reference assays. GAPDH was selected as the internal reference based on its established use in human fibroblasts under comparable experimental conditions. Its Cq values showed no statistically significant difference in GAPDH expression between control and CO-IBZ-treated samples, supporting its use for normalization in the present study. Experiments were conducted using five biological replicates per condition, each analyzed in technical triplicate. Technical replicates were averaged within each biological replicate before statistical analysis, and statistical comparisons were performed using biological replicates as the experimental unit. This approach was adopted to avoid pseudoreplication and to ensure that statistical inference reflected biological rather than intra-assay technical variability. 2.5. Immunofluorescence Analysis Immunofluorescence microscopy was used to evaluate signals associated with SIRT1 , NOX4 , PARP1, vWF, and collagen type III following REAC CO-IBZ treatment. Cells were fixed, permeabilized, and incubated overnight at 4 °C under gentle agitation with primary antibodies against SIRT1 (Cell Signaling, Danvers, MA, USA), NOX4 (Santa Cruz Biotechnology, Dallas, TX, USA), PARP1 (Cell Signaling, Danvers, MA, USA), vWF (Santa Cruz Biotechnology, Dallas, TX, USA), and collagen type III (Abcam, Cambridge, UK). After washing, fluorescence-conjugated secondary antibodies (Life Technologies, Grand Island, NY, USA) were incubated for 1 h at 37 °C in the dark. Nuclei were counterstained with DAPI (1 μg/mL) (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Grand Island, NY, USA). Fluorescence images were acquired using a THUNDER Imager DMi8 microscope (Leica, Nussloch, Germany). Standardized exposure parameters, illumination intensity, and acquisition settings were maintained across samples to ensure comparability. Images were selected based on technical quality and representativeness rather than signal intensity. A standardized ImageJ/Fiji analysis procedure was applied consistently across control and treated samples. Quantitative immunofluorescence was expressed as mean fluorescence intensity, and measurements associated with each biological replicate were summarized as a single replicate-level value before statistical comparison. Data are presented as mean ± SD ( n = 5 biological replicates). The primary antibodies were used at the following working dilutions: SIRT1 , 1:100; NOX4 , 1:500 from a 1:50 stock solution; PARP1, 1:800; vWF, 1:500 from a 1:50 stock solution; and collagen type III, 1:500. The corresponding fluorescence-conjugated secondary antibodies were used at 1:500. The RT-qPCR and quantitative immunofluorescence panels provided complementary marker-level readouts rather than a complete set of matched transcript–protein measurements. SIRT1 and NOX4 were assessed using both methods, whereas the remaining markers provided distinct readouts within the vascular-support, cell-cycle and stress-response, DNA-damage-response, and extracellular matrix-related domains. 2.6. Total Antioxidant Capacity Total antioxidant capacity (TAC) in the culture medium after REAC CO-IBZ treatment was measured using the Total Antioxidant Capacity Colorimetric Assay Kit (Abcam, Cambridge, UK). Briefly, 100 μL of Cu 2+ working solution was added to each standard and sample well. The plate was incubated at room temperature for 90 min on an orbital shaker and protected from light, according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Absorbance was measured at 570 nm using a microplate reader (Akribis Scientific, Common Farm, UK). TAC for each sample was calculated as (Ts/Sv) × D, where Ts is the TAC amount in the sample well derived from the standard curve (nmol), Sv is the sample volume added to the well (μL), and D is the sample dilution factor. Assays were conducted using five independent biological replicates per condition, with technical replicates used to assess analytical consistency. 2.7. Nitrite Measurement The Griess Reagent Kit for Nitrite Determination (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) was used to assess changes in nitric oxide (NO)-related nitrite levels after REAC CO-IBZ treatment. According to the assay protocol, 20 μL of Griess Reagent were added to 150 μL of nitrite-containing samples and incubated for 30 min at room temperature. A microplate reader (Akribis Scientific, Common Farm, Barrow, UK) was used to measure absorbance at 548 nm, and values were analyzed relative to the photometric reference sample. Assays were conducted using five independent biological replicates per condition, with technical replicates used to assess analytical consistency. 2.8. Intracellular ROS Measurements Intracellular ROS levels were assessed using a CM-H 2 DCFDA fluorescence probe (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). The dye was reconstituted in DMSO according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Following treatment, cells were incubated with CM-H2DCFDA at a final concentration of 10 µM for 30 min at 37 °C in the dark. After incubation, the dye was removed, the cells were washed with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), and fluorescence was measured in fresh PBS using a microplate reader (Akribis Scientific, Common Farm, Barrow, UK; excitation: 485 nm; emission: 530 nm). Assays were conducted using five independent biological replicates per condition, with technical replicates used to assess analytical consistency. 2.9. Statistical Analysis Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 9.0 software (GraphPad, San Diego, CA, USA). Data normality was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Comparisons between untreated control and REAC CO-IBZ-treated cultures were performed using an unpaired two-tailed Welch’s t -test. Biological replicates were considered as the experimental unit for all quantitative analyses. Technical replicates were averaged within each biological replicate before statistical analysis and were used exclusively to assess analytical consistency. Relative gene expression was calculated using the 2 −ΔΔCt method and is presented as fold change relative to untreated controls. Statistical comparisons were performed at the biological-replicate level using the replicate values generated by the RT-qPCR analysis after averaging the corresponding technical triplicates. Quantitative statistical analysis was also applied to immunofluorescence mean fluorescence intensity, total antioxidant capacity, NO-related nitrite levels, and intracellular ROS datasets. Representative immunofluorescence images are shown together with the corresponding quantitative analysis. Data are reported as mean ± SD. A p -value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.
