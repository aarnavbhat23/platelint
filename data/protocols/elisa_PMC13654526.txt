## Step-by-step method details
Step-by-step method details Guide RNA design and cloning of AAV-CRISPR plasmids Timing: 2–3 weeks This section covers annotation of DNA sequences for gRNA selection, gRNA design, and cloning into the AAV-CRISPR vector. Plasmid annotations completed here are important at later steps for primer design and for editing analysis. The gRNAs are placed in the first half of the coding sequence of the gene of interest, using a combination of manual annotation and online tools to ensure specificity and compatibility with the AAV-CRISPR system for gRNA expression. Finally the gRNAs are ordered as oligonucleotides with overhangs complementary to restriction sites in the AAV-CRISPR cloning vector. Cloning is performed using standard sticky-end cloning with T4 DNA ligase, and the completed plasmids can then be used for AAV production. 1. Download and annotate the mRNA and genomic files for the gene of interest ( Figure 1 ) from NCBI or UCSC Genome Browser. For simplicity, here are the instructions for NCBI: a. Download known mRNA variant files and the genomic file as Genbank format to retain annotations for use in SnapGene or other software. b. Align mRNA files and identify early coding exons in the first half of the gene that are common across expressed isoforms in the liver. Note: For smaller genes with one or two exons, the whole coding region should be considered for CRISPR gRNA design with special emphasis on essential domains. c. Annotate individual exons in the genomic DNA file and define exons within the coding region. d. Annotate repetitive or conserved regions, like signal peptides, that will be common across many proteins. These regions should be avoided for gRNA design. 2. Design gRNAs specific to the gene of interest using software that can predict specificity. Note: We have previously covered these steps, 15 but provide an updated protocol here. The design pipeline uses CRISPOR, 16 but many of these steps can be applied to other design suites. CRITICAL: Design for the smaller Staphylococcus aureus Cas9 uses different predictors and a different protospacer adjacent motif (PAM), NNGRRT, than the widely used (and larger) Streptococcus pyogenes Cas9, which commonly uses a NGG PAM. Ensure that the parameters for the correct gene editor are utilized for gRNA design. a. Identify coding exons within the first half of the coding region that contain PAM motifs for SaCas9, NNGRRT. b. Copy and paste a candidate exon into CRISPOR https://crispor.gi.ucsc.edu/ and execute the search: i. Label the gene name and exon number on the search page for easier identification later. ii. Select the correct genome (e.g., Mus Musculus UCSC.2011). iii. Select the correct PAM: 21 basepair (bp)-NGG(A/G) (A/G)T – Cas9 S. aureus. Note: This protocol centers around a 21 bp gRNA length, but it should be effective for other gRNA conformations where the CRISPR disrupts double-stranded DNA. Select the appropriate CRISPR system for the required application. Note: Other SaCas9 variants exist which have relaxed NNNRRT 17 or NNG 18 PAMs. They can be designed in similar fashion, but with alternate constraints for gRNA selection. c. CRISPOR will generate a list of potential gRNAs. Consider gRNAs that the program highlights in green with the following score parameters: i. Specificity Score: 75+. ii. Efficiency Score: 75+. iii. Out of Frame: 50+. Note: A high specificity score suggests fewer off-target events while a high efficiency score reflects gene disruption is likely to occur rapidly at that gRNA site. The out of frame score increases confidence that the targeted disruption will shift the reading frame to generate a premature stop codon. Note: Since 2024, the efficiency score in CRISPOR is unavailable for SaCas9. This information is left here in case it returns in a future CRISPOR update. In general, a high specificity score is sufficient to select effective gRNAs. d. Further narrow selected gRNA list: i. CRISPOR performs in silico off target analysis. Exclude gRNAs with off-target sites in exons or without mismatches near the PAM. ii. Exclude gRNAs labeled as, “Not with U6/U3” as those gRNAs have quadruple T repeats which terminate transcription from U6. iii. Avoid repeats of three or more of the same nucleotide within ten bases of the protospacer adjacent motif. CRITICAL: For the vector Addgene #109314, 1 , 8 a 5′ G is not included downstream of the U6 promoter. To ensure successful expression of the gRNA from the U6 promoter, if a G is not present in the gRNA sequence, one should be added to the 5′ end (furthest from the PAM), generating a 22 bp gRNA. Note: Selecting at least one high-scoring gRNA with a natural 5′ G is advisable in case adding the G negatively impacts gRNA processing. 3. Annotate 3–5 selected gRNAs in the genomic and mRNA sequence files, ensuring that gRNAs have been selected in more than one exon when possible. Label each gRNA and PAM to clearly indicate whether the gRNA is on the top or bottom strand ( Figure 1 ). Note: The selected gRNAs may not be the top scoring gRNAs after considering off targets and other features in the gRNA list. Note: Annotating gRNA location in the genomic and mRNA files will facilitate amplicon design for DNA and RNA analysis in Steps 18–22. 4. Generate plasmid files containing the gRNA ( Figure 2 ). a. In the plasmid file for the vector the gRNA will be cloned into ( Figure 2 A), identify the BbsI restriction sites. The gRNA will be placed in between the cut sites as shown in Figure 2 B. CRITICAL: The vector used here (Addgene #109314 1 , 8 ) uses BbsI for gRNA cloning. Other vectors use different restriction sites, such as BsmbI or BsaI. b. Orient the gRNA from 5′ to 3′ so that the PAM would be 3′ from the gRNA, but do not insert the PAM into the plasmid file. CRITICAL: If the gRNA does not contain a 5′ G, one should be added in the plasmid file at this step. c. If the gRNA originally exists on the bottom strand of the DNA, the sequence must be inserted as reverse complement in the 5′–3′ direction. 5. Order oligonucleotides for gRNA cloning for the top 3 gRNAs. Note: Oligonucleotides can be ordered from any commercial company, such as Integrated DNA Technologies or Sigma Aldrich. a. Order the “Top” oligonucleotide with a 5′ CACC overhang: 5′ CACC NNNNNNNNNNNNNNNNNNNNN-3′ b. Order the “Bottom” oligonucleotide with a 5′ AAAC overhang. This is the reverse complement of the gRNA plus CAAA: 5′ AAAC NNNNNNNNNNNNNNNNNNNNN-3′ CRITICAL: If the addition of a 5′ G is necessary, the overhang for the Top Oligonucleotide should be 5′-CACCG-gRNA-3′ leading up to the gRNA, and the Bottom Oligonucleotide 5′-gRNA-GCAAA-3′ CRITICAL: Use the correct overhangs to generate the sticky ends if a different cloning site is used. The bases may not be CACC or CAAA for other plasmids. Note: By making the plasmid file first, the Top and Bottom oligonucleotides can simply be copied from the plasmid file to prevent mistakes transcribing the sequence. The Bottom oligonucleotide must be copied as reverse complement from 5′ to 3’. 6. Linearize the destination plasmid with BbsI-HF to prepare the cut vector for cloning. a. Set up the 50 μL digest mix: Reagent Amount AAV Destination Plasmid 6–8 μg 10× NEB Cutsmart Buffer 5 μL Water Adjust total volume to 48 μL BbsI-HF (NEB) 2 μL b. Perform the restriction digest using a thermocycler: Steps Temperature Time Incubation 37°C 2–4 h Heat inactivation 65°C 15 min Hold 4°C indefinitely CRITICAL: Do not use Alkaline Phosphatase to dephosphorylate the cut sites on the plasmid. The ligation reaction will require the phosphate groups left on the cut plasmid, since the commercially purchasedoligonucleotides are not ordered with phosphorylation at the 5′ and 3′ ends to reduce cost. 7. Purify the BbsI-cut plasmid vector. a. Add DNA loading dye to the digest and separate cut vector on a 0.9% agarose gel. b. Gel purify the linearized vector using a gel extraction kit and elute in nuclease free water. CRITICAL: Elute the DNA in water instead of the provided elution buffer because many elution buffers in kits contain EDTA, which can interfere with ligations and downstream assays. Note: We use the Qiagen gel extraction kit but have achieved similar success with other commercial kits. c. Measure the DNA concentration of the purified cut vector using a nanodrop spectrophotometer. d. Dilute the cut vector to 25 ng/μL. 8. Prepare solutions for Oligonucleotide Annealing and cloning into BbsI site. a. Make 10× Annealing Buffer. b. Dilute Oligonucleotides to 100 μM in nuclease free water. Vortex to mix. c. Dilute the 100 μM primer stocks 1:100 in TE buffer to 1 μM final concentration (5 μL 100 μM oligonucleotide stock + 495 μL TE). Vortex to mix. 9. Prepare annealed oligonucleotides: a. Set up 100 μL annealing reaction in 1.5 mL Eppendorf tubes: Reagent Amount 10× Annealing Buffer 10 μL 1 μM “top” oligonucleotide 8 μL 1 μM “bottom” oligonucleotide 8 μL Water 74 μL b. Vortex, and then centrifuge briefly to bring all liquid to the bottom of the tube. c. Heat a beaker filled 2/3 of the way with water for 5–7 min in the microwave until boiling. d. While the water heats, secure the tubes containing the annealing mix into foam floats. e. Float the oligonucleotides in the boiling water (100°C) and place at the bench. f. The water will cool slowly to 25°C, annealing the oligonucleotides. Centrifuge briefly to spin down condensation and store the annealed oligonucleotides at −20°C until ready for use. Note: A thermocycler can be used for this step, heating at 95°C for 5 min, followed by a slow cooling ramp of 1°C/min. 10. Set up oligo ligations with 50 ng of linearized vector (not dephosphorylated). a. Plan for a single ligation for each annealed gRNA oligonucleotide pair, as well as a vector with no insert with and without ligase. b. Set up the 20 μL ligation mixes: Reagent Amount 10× T4 Ligase Buffer 2 μL 50 ng Cut Vector (25 ng/μL) 2 μL Annealed oligonucleotides 2 μL Water 13 μL T4 Ligase 1 μL c. Incubate ligations at 16°C for 4–5 h. Freeze ligation at −20°C or proceed directly to transformation. Note: Most ligases state that 15 min at 25°C is sufficient, but a longer incubation at a colder temperature yields less background. We have had success using the longer ligation described above as well as a 15 min 25°C ligation. 11. Transform ligations, including ligation negative, uncut plasmid vector positive control, and water controls, into chemically competent E. coli using standard protocols with 12–16h incubation. a. Thaw competent bacteria on ice for 10 min. b. Aliquot 50 μL aliquots into 1.5 mL microcentrifuge tubes. c. Add 5 μL of ligated DNA to each tube, and 10 ng of uncut plasmid vector positive control to the control tube. Add water to a negative control tube. d. Incubate on ice for 10 min. e. Heat shock for 45 s at 42°C. f. Ice shock for 2 min. g. Add 450 μL of sterile, antibiotic-free Luria Broth (LB) to each transformation. h. Incubate shaking at 37°C for 45 min. i. Plate using sterile technique. Note: We use NEB Stables for transformation as this E. coli strain has additional deleted nuclease genes that limit mutagenesis of the repetitive AAV inverted terminal repeats (ITRs). CRITICAL: From the time of transformation, the protocol should be completed through Maxiprep within 10 days. Longer periods increase the chances of mutagenized ITRs, which will not allow AAV packaging, or result in death of the bacterial culture, which will prevent propagation of the plasmid. Note: NEB Stables grow more slowly than other more commonly used strains of bacteria, so they need more time to grow visible colonies, typically 20 h. 12. Select three colonies for mini prep and isolate DNA using commercial kits (e.g., QIAGEN mini prep kit). 13. Confirm successful cloning of the gRNA and intact AAV ITRs with whole plasmid sequencing. Note: Digests can be completed as below as an alternative to whole plasmid sequencing ( Figure 2 C), but may miss smaller deletions in the ITRs. Restriction digest should be used as only a rough structural check with whole plasmid sequencing being used for final verification. a. Digest ∼500 ng of miniprep DNA with single digests of BbsI-HF, XmaI, SnaBI-HF, and PvuII-HF in 20 μL reactions to confirm insertion of the gRNA (noncutting of BbsI) and intactness of the ITRs. Include an uncut control with no enzyme ( Figure 2 C). i. Set up the 20 μL digest mix: Reagent Amount Miniprep Plasmid (100 ng/μL or higher) 5 μL 10× NEB Cutsmart Buffer 2 μL Water Adjust total volume to 19 μL BbsI-HF, XmaI, SnaBI-HF, or PvuII-HF 1 μL ii. Perform the restriction digest: Steps Temperature Time Incubation 37°C 2–4 h Heat inactivation 65°C 15 min Hold 4°C forever CRITICAL: Include the same miniprep control incubated with digest buffer but no enzyme. Because DNA migrates higher in the gel when it is cut by restriction enzyme, this control confirms the BbsI sites can no longer be cut. Note: In rare cases, some gRNAs will retain the BbsI cut site. This can be identified when building the plasmid files in Step 4. CRITICAL: The XmaI, SnaBI, and PvuII digests cut within the ITR region that would be packaged in the AAV. If the restriction digest does not look like the predicted digest from SnapGene or Benchling, that plasmid is unlikely to be correctly packaged into AAV as the ITRs are mutated. That culture should be discarded and a different culture should be selected for downstream analysis. An example of a digest following ITR mutation is shown in Figure 2 . 14. From the digest or sequence-verified culture, grow a 250 mL maxiprep culture in LB with 100 μg/mL Ampicillin for at least 20 h. a. Purify with a maxiprep purification kit. b. Record the concentration of the final AAV-CRISPR plasmid and store at −20°C until AAV transfection. 15. Primer design for quantification of DNA editing Timing: 2 h The goal of this section is to design primers to amplify a ∼400 bp region surrounding the gRNA cut site. These primers are used for both DNA-SV qPCR and Sanger sequencing analysis for INDEL analysis and can be annotated on the gRNA design files generated in Steps 1–4. 18. Open the genomic DNA file with annotations for the gRNAs that were designed in Steps 1–4. 19. Highlight the area 300 bp upstream and 300 bp downstream of the gRNA site. 20. Input this 622 bp sequence into NCBI Primer Blast and select the following parameters: a. Select minimum 375 and maximum 450 bp for PCR Product Size. b. Set database to, “Genomes for selected eukaryotic organisms,” and input “mus musculus.” c. Select “Show results in a new window,” and initiate via “Get Primers.” 21. Select primer pairs that are at least 125 bp away from the gRNA site on each side which have high specificity. Note: Consider the predicted size of each off-target amplicon – a 3 kb will not easily amplify under fast qPCR conditions, but 300 bp off targets more likely will amplify if the primer matches well. 22. Choose two different specific primer sets that do not overlap in binding site to maximize amplification of the target region. Use the following selection criteria: a. Avoid repetitive regions, especially 4 or more of same base. b. No more than 3 out of the last five 3′ bases should be C or G. c. Target a T m 57°C–62°C with GC content about 50%. Note: If no suitable primers return, increase the window for DNA bases selected from the genomic DNA sequence. Alternatively, primers can be designed by hand but should be verified for specificity using Primer BLAST. 23. Test the primers for efficiency using the standards and setup for the qPCR protocol below (Steps 75–76). 24. Order oligonucleotides for testing the gRNAs which will be tested via AAV. Note: Oligonucleotides can be ordered from any commercial company, such as Integrated DNA Technologies or Sigma Aldrich. Pause point: The protocol can be paused at this point indefinitely. In vivo pilot experiment to test AAV-CRISPR Timing: 10–14 days This section describes the design and execution of the in vivo pilot experiment to test the effectiveness of the newly prepared AAV-CRISPR to disrupt the gene of interest. It includes considerations for experimental design, as well as notes on mouse strain, sex, and route of AAV administration. 25. Design the CRISPR tester experiment with at least n=3 mice/group, including a control group ( Figure 3 A). Note: It is helpful to have more control mice than experimental mice as the increased number of control animals allows for tighter gene editing analysis. We often use n=5–6 mice for the control group due to the need for efficient control amplification for later DNA qPCR analysis. Note: This protocol has been optimized for C57BL/6J mice. It may be applicable to other mouse strains but testing AAV transduction using a fluorescent reporter such as GFP should be used to determine dosing required for efficient liver targeting. CRITICAL: Mice should be at least 8 weeks old at time of AAV-CRISPR testing. Gene editing with AAV-CRISPR occurs over a period of days to weeks. In younger animals, the liver has a larger proportion of dividing hepatocytes. Since episomal AAV genomes are lost or diluted with cell division, this can lead to lower editing rates in younger animals. 26. Prepare AAV-CRISPR and PBS control doses in a biosafety cabinet. a. Dilute AAV to 5×10 11 genome copies per 100 μL of sterile PBS in a biosafety cabinet. b. Prepare 10% excess to account for the void volume in syringes. c. Prepare an adequate amount of PBS with no virus for the control injections. Note: Adeno associated virus is typically classed at biosafety level 1. Wipe surfaces with 10% bleach solution followed by 70% ethanol. Dispose of all materials that have contacted virus in biohazard waste. 27. In the mouse facility, prepare a syringe with 100 μL of the virus or PBS to be injected. 28. Identify the mouse and administer the prepared virus via intraperitoneal injection ( Figure 3 B). a. Use a scruff restraint, ensuring the mouse is held securely and safely. b. Deliver the virus in the lower left quadrant, ensuring that both the skin and abdominal wall have been punctured with the needle. c. Pull back on the syringe to ensure no blood vessels have been punctured. d. Remove the syringe and place in a biohazard sharps container. Note: With the mouse still restrained, inspect the abdomen to inspect the injection site for subcutaneous swelling. If swelling is observed, this suggests that the AAV was delivered between the abdominal wall and skin and the liver will not be transduced with AAV, constituting a failed injection. Note: Intraperitoneal injections are very efficient for AAV2/8 delivery to the liver. 20 Intravenous injections via the tail vein or retroorbital plexus, which are more difficult to perform can also be used, but are not necessary for this AAV serotype. Dosages for other injection routes and serotypes may need to be optimized. Note: It has been previously reported that AAV delivery is less efficient in female mice, 21 , 22 but this is contrary to our experience where we still achieve efficient AAV delivery to the liver. For 8-week old female mice that have not been exposed to males, most are not yet in estrous cycling and can be injected by intraperitoneal injection like males of the same age. For older females, or females which have been confirmed to be cycling, we inject the day after estrous and observe efficient AAV delivery. e. Return the mouse to its cage and complete observations at 24, 48, and 72 h, including a cage change at 72 h post-injection. Note: We change cages 72 h after AAV injection as most virus is cleared from the body by that time. This allows disposal of any potentially infective virus that may have been shed. 29. Ten to fourteen days after AAV-CRISPR injection, harvest the liver for editing analysis ( Figure 3 C). a. Anesthetize and euthanize mice following approved methods of euthanasia. Optional: Bleed mice to clear the liver of blood, then proceed to euthanasia by cervical dislocation and thoracotomy. b. Dissect open the abdominal cavity and remove the whole liver by grasping the connective tissue that joins all the lobes and excising surrounding tissue away. c. Excise the largest lobe into several small pieces, estimating the size between 20–50 mg. Place the pieces around the inside of a microcentrifuge tube so that they are not touching and snap freeze in liquid nitrogen ( Figure 3 C). Note: Placing the tissue pieces into the tube so it is not touching allows for selection of pieces for DNA, RNA, and possibly protein analysis without re-cutting frozen tissues. d. Snap freeze remaining liver tissue in a separate tube. e. Store the collected liver tissue at −80°C until analysis. Pause point: The protocol can be paused at this point until DNA analysis is performed. Figure 3 In vivo testing of AAV-CRISPR (A) Experimental design of a pilot AAV-CRISPR experiment. At least two gRNAs are recommended for testing of each new target. (B) Intraperitoneal injection. Assessment of hepatic DNA-level structural variants Timing: 3 h This section describes a qPCR-based method that captures DNA structural variants (SVs). Measuring DNA-SVs on liver DNA using qPCR takes advantage of the inability of most polymerases to amplify large, multiple kilobase DNA inserts in a short period of time. Thus, there is a reduction in the amplification of the DNA SV region when compared to non-edited amplification of the same region, which can then be used to quantify a percentage of DNA structural variant events, including AAV integrations ( Figure 6 A). 75.
