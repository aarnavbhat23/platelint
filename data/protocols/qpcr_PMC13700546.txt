## 2. Materials and Methods
2. Materials and Methods A detailed list of all materials used can be found in Supplementary Tables S1–S5 . 2.1. Collection and Cleaning Umbilical cords were collected from N = 32 warmblood mares and foals in one stud farm. The foals’ sex and whether they were carried by a surrogate mare via embryo transfer or by their mother were considered as best as possible before sampling; however, depending on the timing of foaling, even allocation to the groups was not always possible. Therefore, half of all samples taken were embryo transfer foals from surrogate mares and half were natural pregnancies. Of samples taken, 57% were from colts and 43% from fillies. The mares’ ages averaged at 9.8 ± 5 years. Samples were taken in accordance with the German animal welfare legislation (see section Institutional Review Board Statement; reference number 7221.3-14163_19306_23). Mares and foals were included in the study after a physiological pregnancy and parturition. Samples were collected after complete foetal membrane expulsion in order to not interrupt the mare–foal bonding. Umbilical cord samples of 20 cm were collected from the allantoic portion near the placenta (as defined previously [ 45 ]), avoiding areas damaged during or after birth. The samples were cut with sterile scalpel blades and immediately cleaned under running 4 °C tap water for 5 to 10 min, until macroscopically clean. Afterwards, the samples were disinfected [ 17 ], with some protocol adaptations. First, the samples were submerged in 70% ethanol (Carl Roth GmbH + Co. KG, Karlsruhe, Germany) for 3 min, constantly agitated to ensure even coverage in ethanol of all crevices; next, they were air-dried and then submerged in 1% iodine solution (B. Braun Vet Care GmbH, Tuttlingen, Germany) for 3 min, again constantly kept in motion. Finally, the samples were thoroughly rinsed in sterile phosphate buffered saline without Ca 2+ and Mg 2+ (PBS (−/−); Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., Waltham, MA, USA) until the solution was clear. Each sample was then placed in a sterile bag and submerged in 500 mL of sterile buffered transport solution as developed by Passeri et al. [ 27 ] and modified by Burk et al. [ 17 ], containing PBS (−/−) with 500 IU/mL penicillin, 0.5 mg/mL streptomycin (Sigma-Aldrich ® , Merck KGaA, Darmstadt, Germany), 2.5 μg/mL amphotericin B (Corning Inc., Corning, NY, USA) and 0.05 mg/mL gentamycin (Carl Roth GmbH + Co. KG, Karlsruhe, Germany) (5% Pen/Strep, 1% Amp B, and 0.1% Genta). The bag was sealed airtight, double bagged, and then heavily agitated to ensure that all tissues were evenly and completely submerged in antimicrobials. The samples were then stored overnight at 4 °C for 12 h to reduce microbial contamination; overnight storage time was half the duration of the original protocol [ 27 ]. 2.2. Tissue Preparation and Enzyme Digestion After 12 h, samples were processed further by being thoroughly rinsed in a fresh sterile transport solution, isolating vessels, and preparing the tissue ( Figure 1 b,c) as previously published [ 43 , 46 ]. The perivascular Wharton’s jelly was cleaned from vessels, intervascular Wharton’s jelly, and endothelium and rinsed again. Tissue samples were finely minced with sterile scissors into 1–3 mm 3 cubes, then 10.5 ± 1.0 g were transferred into 50 mL centrifuge tubes and submerged in enzyme solution (2 mL/g). The enzyme solution was composed of low-glucose DMEM (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., Waltham, MA, USA) and collagenase I (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., Waltham, MA, USA) at 500 U/mL (2 to 2.5 mg/mL, depending on the batch of collagenase I) [ 17 , 33 ]. For each pair (RM/NM), samples were digested using the same batch of collagenase I. The tubes were set in a water bath at 37 °C for 3 h (RM) or 24 h (NM) and vortexed regularly. After the incubation period, all samples were first filtered through 2 mm sterile metal mesh (Fackelmann GmbH Co. KG, Hersbruck, Germany), rinsed with PBS (−/−), centrifuged (600× g , room temperature, 10 min), and filtered through a 100 μm cell strainer (Corning Inc., Corning, NY, USA), then rinsed and centrifuged again. After one final round of washing, the cell pellet was resuspended in the basic cell culture medium, containing low-glucose DMEM, 20% foetal bovine serum (FBS) (Sigma-Aldrich ® , Merck KGaA, Darmstadt, Germany), 1% Pen/Strep, 1% Amp B and 0.1% Genta [ 17 ]. However, we increased the Amp B concentration to 1% instead of 0.2% to account for the reduced antifungal activities in the presence of larger serum quantities [ 47 ] and seeded cells into one T75 cell culture flask (TPP Techno Plastic Products AG, Trasadingen, Switzerland). A total of 64 samples of cells were collected from the same segment of perivascular Wharton’s jelly tissue to compare both digestion methods. To minimise individual-dependant bias, 3 h (RM) and 24 h (NM) samples were always taken from the same foals for paired comparison. 2.3. Experimental Design The RM and NM for equine uc-MSC isolation were compared using a paired, non-inferiority framework for this pilot study. All samples were decontaminated and disinfected in one piece and tissue was allocated to each method after mincing to ensure baseline comparability ( Figure 2 ). Created in BioRender. Hollender, A. (2026) https://BioRender.com/snz35os . 2.4. Cell Culture The cells were cultured at 37 °C with 5% CO 2 in a vapour-saturated incubator. Twenty-four hours after seeding, cells were washed three times with PBS with Ca 2+ and Mg 2+ (PBS (+/+); Carl Roth GmbH + Co. KG, Karlsruhe, Germany) and then further cultured with medium replacements and microscopic checks twice per week until 80–90% confluence. For passaging, cells were detached with 0.25%/0.02% trypsin/EDTA (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., Waltham, MA, USA) for 10 to 15 min and split 1:3; they were then frozen after passage 2 (P2) in cryopreservation medium containing low-glucose DMEM, 40% FBS, 10% dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) (AppliChem GmbH, Darmstadt, Germany), 1% Pen/Strep, 1% Amp B, and 0.1% Genta, modified as published before [ 29 , 47 , 48 ] at 2 million cells per mL in Mr. Frosty™ (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., Waltham, MA, USA) at −80 °C for at least one day and then in liquid nitrogen for permanent storage. The isolation success was scored by microscopy (MOTICEUROPE, S.L.U., Barcelona, Spain) [ 48 ], with either 0 points for no cell attachment and 1 point for cell attachment before passage 1 (P1). In addition, within the first microscopic examination after isolation the number of cells attached was scored; 0 points for no cells, 1 point for single cells within the average field of view (FOV), 2 points for small island formations with up to 10 cells, and 3 points for larger islands with more than 10 cells per island. Contamination was scored separately; if the cell culture was clean until the first passaging after seeding, it was considered “not contaminated” with a score of 0; if a fungal or bacterial contamination appeared within passage 0 (P0) after seeding, it was counted as contaminated with a score of 1, due to the initial tissue being contaminated. Contamination detected after the first passaging was not taken into consideration. As the RM and NM protocols were compared in a paired study design, both samples from that donor were excluded from subsequent experiments whenever one of them was contaminated and therefore discarded. 2.5. Characterisation All samples were characterised as first suggested by the International Society for Cellular Therapy (ISCT) [ 49 ] and then further specified for equine stem cells by De Schauwer et al. [ 50 ] and Guest et al. [ 51 ] with a modified flow cytometry panel. Out of the remaining 28 paired samples, 8 were lost due to contamination in later passages after an autoclave failure and 5 more after technical cryostorage malfunctions, leading to only 15 of 32 paired samples usable for flow cytometry and RT-qPCR ( N = 15). All further characterisation experiments, including trilineage differentiation and cell proliferation assays, were conducted using paired samples of three foals ( N = 3) in P4 after cryopreservation. The cells were examined for fibroblast-like growth patterns and spindle-like shape as well as their ability to adhere to the plastic culture vessel. 2.6. Inter-Passage Time and Population-Doubling Time To compare both methods, the culture duration between passages in days (inter-passage time (IPT) in d) for N = 20 paired samples was calculated from isolation until cryopreservation, including the mean and standard deviation per method and passage (P0 to P1; P1 to P2; P2 to cryopreservation). For three paired ( N = 3) samples in P4, a population-doubling assay was performed [ 52 , 53 ]. In short, 30,000 cells/well (3.5 cm 2 ) were sown into 12-well plates on day zero (T 0 ), and each day three wells per sample were harvested and counted in duplicate with the Automated Cell Counter (Bio-Rad Laboratories GmbH, Feldkirchen, Germany). The settings were previously established by Stage et al. [ 53 ] and confirmed for this type of primary cells via manual counting in the Neubauer chamber. The percentage of dead cells was also counted, and the means were calculated for both values. This was carried out until day 8 (T 8 ). The calculations were carried out utilising Equation (1) [ 52 ], where T is the harvest time of culture, T 0 the starting time of culture, and N T and N 0 are the cell count of harvest and cell count of seeding, respectively. Trilineage Differentiation All differentiation trials were executed in duplicates (chondrogenic induced samples) or triplets (all other samples), using the same three paired samples ( N = 3) as for the PDT. All culture vessels for the adipogenic and osteogenic differentiations were coated with 2 µg/cm 2 of laminin (Corning Inc., Corning, NY, USA) to reduce cell detachment, as previously established [ 53 ]. Negative controls (NCs) were included as triplets at 20,000 cells/cm 2 density in laminin-coated 24-well plates (Starlab GmbH, Hamburg, Germany). 2.7.1. Chondrogenic Differentiation Chondrogenic differentiation was induced in 3D pellet culture as published before [ 53 , 54 ]. In brief, cells were thawed, expanded, and passaged and 5 × 10 5 cells were centrifuged into a pellet. The cells were then seeded into uncoated 15 mL centrifuge vials (TPP Techno Plastic Products AG, Trasadingen, Switzerland), covered with 2 mL of chondrogenic differentiation medium (CD medium) [ 53 ], and vortexed for 1 min every day. The CD medium was replaced three times per week; the caps were not screwed on tight to ensure gas exchange in the incubator. The culture was continued until day 21 (T 21 ), when the pellets and the NCs were fixed in 4% formalin (Carl Roth GmbH + Co. KG, Karlsruhe, Germany), the pellets were then embedded in paraffin (A. Menarini Diagnostics, Berlin, Germany) and cut for histological examination (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., Waltham, MA, USA). Alcian blue (Morphisto GmbH, Offenbach am Main, Germany) and haematoxylin–eosin stains (Waldeck GmbH & Co. KG, Münster, Germany) were chosen as established in-house [ 53 ]. Stained slides and NCs were scored blinded with the Bern score by Grogan et al. [ 55 ], modified for Alcian-blue-stained samples [ 30 , 53 ]. Samples scored 0 to 3 points for uniformity as well as matrix and cell morphology, for a maximum of 9 points in total. 2.7.2. Osteogenic Differentiation Osteogenic differentiation was induced as established in-house [ 53 ]. In short, cells were thawed, expanded, passaged and 20,000 cells/cm 2 were seeded in triplets into laminin-coated 24-well plates. The cells were expanded until confluence, covered with 1 mL of osteogenic differentiation medium (OD medium); the medium was changed three times per week. The culture was continued until T 21 , when cells were fixed in 4% formalin. Von Kossa staining (Morphisto GmbH, Offenbach am Main, Germany) and nuclear fast red stains were performed for extracellular calcium deposits and nuclei [ 53 ]. The samples were evaluated via microscope and photographs were examined in a blinded trial. A semi-quantitative scoring system ( Table 1 ) for the evaluation of osteogenic differentiation success and von Kossa staining was developed based on other scoring systems [ 30 , 55 ]. Samples scored 0 to 3 points for coverage of field of view (FOV) by mineral deposits in %, 0 to 3 points for the size of the predominant variety of deposits in relation to cells, since FOVs usually contain a mix of granule sizes, and 0 to 3 points for the arrangement and clustering of mineral deposits, all measured with 10× magnification. In total, a maximum of 9 points can be achieved. The implementation of this system was necessary to keep osteogenic differentiation assessable even if plate reader scoring [ 56 ] or ImageJ scoring [ 57 ] could not be completed due to technical restrictions or overgrowth of the negative controls. Adipogenic Differentiation As specific media components for the established in-house protocol were not available at the time of the experiment, the established protocol was replaced with the Stem Cell Kit for adipogenic differentiation with some added modifications [ 54 ]. In short, cells were thawed, expanded, and passaged and 20,000 cells/cm 2 were seeded in triplets into laminin-coated 24-well plates. Cells were expanded until confluence, covered with 1 mL of adipogenic differentiation medium (AD medium), consisting of a StemPro™ Adipogenesis Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., Waltham, MA, USA) supplemented with 5% rabbit serum (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., Waltham, MA, USA), 1% Pen/Strep, 1% Amp B and 0.1% Genta. The medium was changed every other day; the culture was continued until day 7 (T 7 ), when cells were fixed in 4% formalin. Nile red and DAPI staining was executed as established in-house [ 53 ]. Blinded scoring was then performed as described [ 30 , 54 ], scoring 0 to 3 points for the percentage of differentiated cells and 0 to 3 points for the size and arrangement of lipid droplets. 2.8. Reverse-Transcription Quantitative PCR The cells for reverse-transcription quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR) were detached using Accutase ® (Carl Roth GmbH + Co. KG, Karlsruhe, Germany) to keep them suitable for flow cytometry as well as RNA extraction. One T75 flask of cells in P3 was rinsed with PBS (−/−) and then incubated with 10 mL of Accutase ® according to the manufacturer’s instructions. RNA extraction (Macherey Nagel GmbH & Co. KG, Düren, Germany), cDNA synthesis (Bio-Rad Laboratories GmbH, Feldkirchen, Germany), and SYBR Green (Bio-Rad Laboratories GmbH, Feldkirchen, Germany) RT-qPCR were executed following manufacturers’ instructions, using the ViiA7 Thermocycler (Applied Biosystems/Life Technologies; Foster City, CA, USA) and identical primers plus an additional primer ( Table 2 ) for the cell surface antigen CD90 ( THY-1 ). THY-1 was established using the protocol established in-house [ 48 ]. Glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase ( GAPDH ), β-actin ( ACTB ), and 18S ribosomal RNA ( RN18S ) were employed as housekeepers and POU class 5 homeobox 1 or octamer-binding transcription factor 4 ( POU5F1/OCT4 ), DNA methyltransferase 3 beta ( DNMT3B ), DENN domain containing 4A ( DENND4A ), MYC proto-oncogene, BHLH transcription factor ( MYC ), and the Thy-1 cell surface antigen CD90 ( THY-1 ) were analysed as stemness-associated transcriptional markers. The data were normalised to the three housekeeping genes (ΔCt). All statistical analyses were performed using ΔCt values. Statistical testing of ΔCt values was only performed if the Ct values of the respective samples were more than four cycles apart from the average Ct values of H 2 O and no-reverse-transcriptase controls (–RT controls). Cost-Efficiency Analysis The catalogue prices in euros for all media components necessary for the isolation and cultivation of uc-MSCs were acquired through the manufacturers’ retail websites [ 58 , 59 , 60 , 61 , 62 , 63 , 64 , 65 , 66 , 67 ], with settings and taxes for Germany and no discounts applied (see Table A1 ). All further calculations were conducted using the RM as the baseline, with the NM as the relative difference. Results are therefore reported as percentages (mean ± standard deviation); whereas the prices, currencies, and taxes may vary depending on the economic region as well as supply and demand, the difference between the two methods does not. Defining the price of labour is difficult, since hourly wages differ vastly depending on country, employee, and employer. As a reliable and transparent data baseline for this publication we therefore chose the gross wages in euros defined for personnel employed by the German government. The Tarifvertrag für den öffentlichen Dienst (TVöD) [ 68 ] and the Tarifvertrag für den öffentlichen Dienst der Länder (TV-L) [ 69 ] include clear regulations regarding over- and nighttime pay and a salary scale for employees of state institutions. Specifically, the pay grades ( Entgeltgruppen ) E5 to E14 of laboratory personnel at the basic experience level 1 were used (see Table A2 ) and the median was calculated. As discussed above, all further results are presented as the relative difference between the RM (baseline) and the NM. Critical time points during the protocol process were recorded, times were linearised and centred around midnight to account for the circular nature of time-of-day data and prevent distortion of results. The median (5th percentile; 95th percentile) was then calculated, using midnight as the central reference as equine births usually occur around midnight [ 70 ]. 2.11. Statistical Analysis All values given are means ± standard deviations unless stated differently. All flow cytometry data are presented as medians (IQR; 25th–75th percentile). Normality of the data was visually assessed prior to analysis, using a Q-Q plot. To compare the 3 h method (RM) with our extended 24 h incubation (NM), a paired t -test for non-inferiority analysis was performed with α = 0.05, using NCSS 2026 statistical software (NCSS, LLC. Kaysville, UT, USA) [ 71 , 72 , 73 , 74 ]. Technical replicates were averaged to a single value per donor before analysis, each donor was handled as the experimental unit ( N = donors). All further paired tests were performed with these averaged values. The aim of the present study was to develop a method that is not inferior in the categories inter-passage time (IPT), population-doubling time (PDT), trilineage potential, and gene expression. Since the immunophenotype data after flow cytometry were skewed either to the right or left, we performed the non-inferiority analysis using the non-parametric Wilcoxon signed-rank test with continuity correction and α = 0.05. For all ordinal data from the trilineage assays, differentiation scores were likewise assessed using the one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test on donor-level paired differences; exact p values are reported where computable and the normal approximation with continuity correction was used in all other cases (i.e., in the presence of ties). Paired effect estimates and corresponding confidence intervals for non-parametric data were calculated as the Hodges–Lehmann estimate, with 95% confidence intervals (wilcox.test, conf.int = TRUE, R, Supplementary Listing S1 ). The p values from the non-inferiority tests for IPT, PDT, and trilineage potential were corrected for multiple testing using the Holm method (p.adjust, R, Supplementary Listing S2 ) at a familywise α of 0.05. Calculated p values for the RT-qPCR and flow cytometry results (five markers each) were also corrected using the Holm method at a familywise α of 0.05. In summary, the null hypothesis for each category was that the NM is not non-inferior to the RM. The alternative hypothesis was that the NM is non-inferior to the RM ( Table 4 ). Generally, the margins of non-inferiority for each test were set pragmatically in such a way that the performance of the NM uc-MSCs could still be tolerated, ensuring study-specific fitness-for-purpose rather than reflecting biologically or clinically validated equivalence limits. Different aspects were taken into account to ensure the NM uc-MSCs could still fulfil their purpose in this in vitro pilot study, including tolerable limits of heterogeneity of the cultured cell population, reduction in differentiation potential, and stemness-associated transcriptional markers and antigen effects ( Table 4 ). More specifically, for the downstream in vitro studies planned, a population of cells containing 15% more non-target cells was prespecified as acceptable for the gene expression and immunophenotype, as this variation remains within passage- and donor-dependent variation normally present in primary cell cultures (Equation (2)). (2) ΔCtmax = −log2(0.85) ≈ 0.2345 Equation (2). Prespecified non-inferiority margin for RT-qPCR data analysis. Similarly, a slower PDT (up to 12 h) and a longer IPT (up to 7 d) were prespecified as acceptable, as differences of this magnitude lie within donor-, passage-, and seeding-density-dependent variation in expansion kinetics. For this pilot study, it was the operative requirement that the cells reach a sufficient quantity within one extra week to ensure that applications within the same differentiation assays remained comparable. Finally, a reduction of 0.5 points per subcategory while scoring differentiation success, resulting in a 1-point (adipogenic) or 1.5-point (chondrogenic, osteogenic) decrease, was prespecified as acceptable, as a reduction of this magnitude corresponds at most with a modest reduction in proportion of differentiated cells.
