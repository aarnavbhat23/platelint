1. Synthesize the codon-optimized nucleotide sequence encoding the MEAET antigen as provided in Supplementary Table S1, clone it into a prokaryotic expression vector, and transform into E. coli BL21 (DE3).

2. Culture the positive clone in LB medium with 100 µg/mL ampicillin until OD600 reaches 0.4–0.6, induce expression with 500 µM IPTG at 16 °C and 120 rpm for 18 h, harvest cells, and lyse by sonication.

3. Purify the GST-tagged MEAET protein using glutathione agarose affinity chromatography according to the manufacturer's instructions, remove the GST tag via thrombin digestion, and verify the purified protein by SDS-PAGE and BCA protein assay (store at −80 °C).

4. Prepare hyperimmune serum by emulsifying the purified MEAET protein with ISA 206 VG adjuvant (1:1 volume ratio) and immunizing three 30-day-old SPF piglets via multiple-site intramuscular injection (1 mg primary dose, 2 mg booster doses every 10 days for a total of three immunizations), monitoring health daily, and collecting high-titer serum (>1:102,400) from the anterior vena cava for storage at −40 °C in 0.5 mL aliquots.

5. Perform the indirect ELISA using the purified MEAET antigen as the coating plate: coat 96-well plates with 100 µL/well of MEAET in carbonate-bicarbonate buffer (pH 9.6) at 37 °C for 1 h then 4 °C overnight; wash three times with PBST; block with 200 µL/well of 5% skim milk in PBST at 37 °C for 2 h; and wash three times with PBST.

6. Add our provided clinical serum samples (diluted 1:400 in PBST, 100 µL/well) to the plates, incubate at 37 °C for 1 h, and wash three times with PBST.

7. Add HRP-conjugated goat anti-pig IgG (diluted 1:10,000 in PBST, 100 µL/well), incubate at 37 °C for 1 h, and wash five times with PBST.

8. Add 100 µL of TMB substrate solution to each well, incubate in the dark at 37 °C for 15 min, stop the reaction with 50 µL of 2 M H2SO4 per well, and measure the luminescence using a microplate reader.

9. Evaluate assay sensitivity via two-fold serial dilutions (1:100 to 1:12,800) of three clinical positive serum samples, and test specificity using positive sera against Salmonella spp., Pasteurella multocida, Clostridium perfringens, Listeria monocytogenes, Campylobacter jejuni, Streptococcus suis, non-pathogenic E. coli K-12, PEDV, and PDCoV diluted at 1:200 in triplicate.

10. Establish the cut-off value using the panel of 24 confirmed negative serum samples (calculated as the mean OD450 plus three standard deviations, converted to an S/P ratio, with an S/P ratio ≥ 0.203 designated as positive), and perform statistical analysis on our sample cohorts using SPSS and Python to compare seropositivity rates with a chi-square test.
